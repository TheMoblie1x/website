# Note-Taking, PKM & Knowledge-Management Market — Competitive Landscape

This report consolidates individual research profiles for 94 companies/projects spanning the note-taking and knowledge-management market, mapped across nine segments: B2C Mainstream notes, PKM/Second-Brain "tools for thought," B2B Enterprise Knowledge bases, Developer Documentation, AI Meeting Assistants, Project-Management-Adjacent tools with docs features, AI-Native knowledge apps, Privacy/Local-First tools, and Handwriting/Student apps. It is built entirely from the individual company profiles in `profiles/` (each sourced via web research, with figures labeled "estimated" where not officially disclosed). The purpose is to give a single, scannable view of ownership structure, business model, pricing, financial scale, and competitive position across the whole landscape — useful context for positioning a new Notes product against the full field rather than a handful of obvious rivals.

## Segment Narratives

### B2C Mainstream
This segment splits cleanly into two tiers: free, bundled big-tech defaults (Apple Notes, Google Keep, OneNote, Samsung Notes) that have no standalone P&L and win purely on distribution, and independent paid apps competing for the remainder. Notion is the outright financial winner of the whole B2C tier (~$600M ARR, $10-11B valuation) by having escaped "just a notes app" positioning into a full workspace category. Among true indies, Craft, Obsidian, and Bear represent very different bets — Craft is VC-funded but still finding its financial footing after a slow (2025) Android launch, Obsidian is deliberately unfunded and profitable on a narrow monetization base (Sync/Publish add-ons only), and Bear is the smallest viable business in the entire dataset (~$330K ARR, 3 employees) sustained purely by Apple-ecosystem loyalty. Evernote is the clearest cautionary tale: once a ~$1B-valuation category leader, now a distressed asset absorbed into Bending Spoons' roll-up portfolio at a fraction of its former value. Simplenote's March 2026 "active development ended" announcement is a second reminder that free, bundled-goodwill products get deprioritized once a parent's strategic focus shifts.

### PKM / Second-Brain
This is the most crowded and most fragmented segment (23 companies), split between well-funded "AI-native" entrants (Tana, Anytype, Mem) and a long tail of bootstrapped or lightly-funded indie tools (Capacities, Logseq, mymind, Zettlr, Reflect, Kortex). No company here has reached Notion-scale financial outcomes; the largest disclosed funding is Anytype's ~$13-29M and Tana's $25M — both far behind Notion's $343M. Financially strongest: Tana (best-funded, largest pre-launch demand signal at 160K+ waitlist) and Heptabase (small but apparently sustainable at ~$1.2M cumulative revenue on just $2.2M raised). Financially weakest/most exposed: Mem, whose $110M 2022 valuation now looks stretched relative to an undisclosed but clearly modest current scale, prompting a strategic pivot from consumer notes to an "AI chief of staff" positioning; and the various sub-$5M-funded solo/small-team apps (Supernotes, xTiles, Reflect) that survive on founder discipline rather than capital. A recurring theme across founder statements in this segment (Capacities, Logseq, Reflect, mymind, Kortex) is explicit anti-VC positioning, marketed directly to users anxious about PKM tools shutting down after running out of runway — a real anxiety, validated by Height's shutdown in the adjacent PM segment.

### B2B Enterprise Knowledge
Glean is the standout story of the entire 95-company dataset: $300M ARR (May 2026, +89% YoY) and a $7.2B valuation reached via a $150M Series F that came just nine months after a round that had already doubled its valuation to $4.6B. No other company in this research — across any segment — comes close to that combination of growth rate and capital raised. Confluence, bundled inside Atlassian, remains the incumbent distribution powerhouse via Jira cross-sell, with an estimated (not disclosed) $700-900M in product revenue. Beneath those two, the segment is a mix of durable-but-small profitable bootstrapped players (Nuclino, Slab, Helpjuice, Document360 — the latter targeting a credible $10M→$25M ARR path with zero outside funding) and smaller VC-backed tools (Slite, Guru) facing real pressure from Glean-style AI-native entrants eroding the "passive wiki" category from above.

### Developer Documentation
Mintlify is the clear momentum leader — 10x revenue growth in a single year (2024→2025) and a $500M valuation roughly 18 months after its Series A, reflecting investor conviction that AI-native, docs-as-code tooling is displacing incumbents. GitBook and ReadMe, both older and much more lightly funded (GitBook has never raised beyond a ~$940K seed), look structurally exposed to being out-invested. At the other extreme, Docusaurus (Meta-backed, free) and BookStack/MediaWiki (community/nonprofit) exert real downward pricing pressure on the whole category for any team willing to self-host — Wikimedia's own Enterprise API line (+148% YoY) is a notable signal that even nonprofit infrastructure can find a commercial edge monetizing AI-era demand for structured data.

### Meeting AI / AI Notetakers
This is the fastest-moving, highest-capital-intensity segment in the whole dataset. Granola's trajectory is extraordinary: a $1.5B valuation in March 2026, up 6x from $250M less than a year earlier, on a $125M Series C. Fathom's growth was even faster in relative terms (90x revenue growth in two years, $1M→$30M ARR) and culminated in its September 2026 acquisition by Superhuman — a direct signal that productivity-suite consolidators see AI meeting intelligence as a must-own capability rather than a point-solution business. Fireflies.ai is the capital-efficiency outlier, reaching unicorn status on under $20M raised. The segment's structural risk is commoditization from free, bundled incumbents: Zoom AI Companion and Google's native Meet/Gemini tooling are given away to retain platform subscriptions, which every standalone notetaker (Avoma, Grain, Supernormal, tl;dv) must out-differentiate on CRM depth or vertical workflow to survive.

### Project-Management-Adjacent
This segment contains the widest range of financial outcomes in the dataset: from ClickUp's disclosed $300M ARR and Monday.com's 26%+ YoY public-company growth, down to Height's outright shutdown in September 2025. The three public companies here (Asana, Monday.com, Airtable-adjacent-but-private) tell a cautionary story about the category's maturity — Asana's growth has decelerated into low double digits with a compressed market cap, and Airtable's valuation was marked down roughly 66% (from $11.7B to ~$4B) even as its ARR kept growing modestly, reflecting investor skepticism about seat-based SaaS pricing durability in an AI-disruption narrative. Linear is the financial bright spot among private competitors — profitable/cash-flow-positive since 2021, reaching a $1.25B valuation on only $134M raised, a sharp contrast to capital-heavy peers.

### AI-Native
AI-native capability is now a cross-cutting feature rather than a standalone segment — nearly every company profiled above (Notion AI, Tana, Granola, Mem, Reflect, Glean, NotebookLM, Fabric, Recall, mymind, Kortex, ClickUp Brain, Linear Agent) has layered AI onto an existing product category rather than competing as a pure-play "AI notes" company. The two standout financial outcomes tagged AI-native — Glean ($7.2B valuation, enterprise search/agents) and Granola ($1.5B valuation, meeting notes) — both come from teams that identified a workflow-specific AI wedge rather than a generic "AI second brain." Generic AI-organization tools without a specific workflow anchor (Fabric, Recall, Mem's original positioning) show the weakest moats in the entire dataset: their core value proposition (auto-tagging, semantic search, summarization) is increasingly a commodity capability any foundation-model wrapper can replicate.

### Privacy / Local-First
This segment is defined by an explicit anti-cloud-lock-in value proposition, and its companies are disproportionately bootstrapped or lightly funded relative to their cloud-native competitors: Obsidian ($0 raised, profitable), Logseq (~$4.1M seed, ~5 employees), SiYuan and Memos and TriliumNext (pure open-source, $0 revenue by design). Anytype is the best-funded true believer in this space (~$13-29M raised) betting that its peer-to-peer encrypted architecture becomes a durable moat once real-time collaboration matures — a bet that cloud-native rivals (Notion, Tana) can't easily copy without abandoning their own architecture. Standard Notes' 2024 acquisition by Proton AG is the segment's most interesting outcome: rather than raising VC, a privacy-first bootstrapped note app found its distribution answer by being folded into a larger privacy-brand ecosystem instead.

### Handwriting / Student
Goodnotes is the clear category leader by brand, revenue, and user base (30M+ registered users, tens of millions in estimated annual revenue), having successfully transitioned from one-time-purchase to subscription and used the resulting capital to push into AI features. Notability remains a strong #2 via its audio-sync differentiator but is capped by an Apple-only platform strategy. The most financially unusual entrant is Flexcil, which raised an outsized ~$38M PE round (Crescendo Equity Partners) for a niche iPad note-taking app — far more capital than any other player in this ten-company segment, signaling aggressive international-expansion ambitions rather than organic growth. MyScript/Nebo occupies a genuinely different position: rather than competing on consumer brand, it monetizes the underlying handwriting-recognition IP via B2B OEM licensing, giving it a revenue base (~$18M, steadily growing) less exposed to consumer app-store dynamics than any pure consumer app in this set.

## Key Cross-Cutting Findings

**Shutdowns, acquisitions, and pivots identified across the 94 profiles:**

- **Height (project management)** — ceased operations entirely on September 24, 2025, after raising $18.3M (incl. a $14M Series A in 2021). Not acquired; the company shut down outright, citing inability to reach sustainable scale in a crowded PM-tool/AI-agent market.
- **Fathom (meeting AI)** — acquired by Superhuman, announced September 14, 2026, after reaching $30M ARR (90x growth in two years) on $30M raised. A consolidation move as productivity platforms race to bundle "agentic work" capabilities.
- **Coda (docs/database hybrid)** — acquired by Grammarly in an all-stock deal, December 2024, after raising ~$240M and reaching a $1.4B valuation (2021). Grammarly itself rebranded to "Superhuman" in October 2025, so Coda now sits inside the same consolidated entity as Fathom.
- **Standard Notes (privacy notes)** — acquired by Proton AG, April 2024, terms undisclosed. Founder Mo Bitar subsequently left Proton (reported 2025).
- **Evernote (mainstream notes)** — acquired by Bending Spoons, January 2023, for a reported ~$10M enterprise value plus debt — a steep fall from its ~$1B peak valuation (2012). Bending Spoons has since filed for a U.S. IPO (2026 SEC filings).
- **Tettra (B2B wiki)** — acquired by GSoft (Canadian employee-experience company), October 2023, terms undisclosed.
- **Bloomfire (B2B knowledge)** — an unclear ownership/liquidity event around February 2023; the acquiring party is not identified in public records.
- **Simplenote (mainstream notes)** — parent Automattic announced in March 2026 that active development has ended; the product is now maintenance-only.
- **Mem (PKM/AI-native)** — not a shutdown, but a significant strategic pivot: rebuilt as "Mem 2.0" and repositioned from a mass-market consumer "second brain" toward an "AI chief of staff" for professional knowledge workers, following a $23.5M Series A (2022) at a ~$110M valuation that now looks difficult to justify at current apparent scale.

**Major recent funding events (signal of where investor capital is concentrating):**

- **Glean** — $150M Series F (June 2025) at a $7.2B valuation, up from $4.6B just nine months earlier — the largest and fastest valuation escalation in the entire dataset.
- **Granola** — $125M Series C (March 2026) at a $1.5B valuation, up 6x from $250M in under a year.
- **Mintlify** — $45M Series B (April 2026) at a $500M valuation, roughly 18 months after its Series A, on the back of 10x revenue growth in one year.
- **Fireflies.ai** — reached $1B unicorn valuation via a June 2025 employee tender offer, notably without a new primary funding round, on under $20M total raised historically.
- **Tana** — $14M Series A (February 2025) at a ~$100M valuation, backed by a strong VC syndicate (Lightspeed, Northzone) off a 160K+ pre-launch waitlist.

**Valuation contractions (signal of investor caution in mature SaaS categories):**

- **Airtable** — valuation fell from $11.7B (2021 Series F) to an estimated ~$4B by late 2025, a roughly 66% markdown, despite continued (if decelerating) ARR growth.
- **Asana** — public market cap compressed to roughly $2.0-2.3B as of September 2026, down substantially from 2021 highs, amid slowing growth and AI-disruption concerns about seat-based pricing.
- **Monday.com** — market cap down roughly 70%+ from prior-year highs as of September 2026, despite continuing to post ~26-27% YoY revenue growth — one of the starkest growth/valuation disconnects in the dataset, reflecting broad investor skepticism toward seat-based SaaS models generally.

**Rebrands worth noting:** NotebookLM was rebranded "Gemini Notebook" in July 2026 as Google folds it deeper into its core Gemini app; Supernormal briefly rebranded to "Radiant" before reverting to "Supernormal" in February 2026.

## Monetization & Unit Economics

Every one of the 94 profiles was extended with a **Unit Economics & Monetization** section covering primary/secondary revenue source, growth/acquisition model, estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost), estimated LTV (customer lifetime value), implied LTV:CAC ratio, and user demographics. **No company in this dataset discloses actual CAC or LTV** — every number below is a modeled estimate derived from the company's business-model archetype (pricing tier, GTM motion, funding stage), not a reported figure. Full per-company reasoning is in each profile's own Unit Economics section.

### Headline finding: advertising is absent as a business model

**0 of 94 companies** in this market run primarily on advertising. Across B2C consumer apps, B2B SaaS, open-source projects, and AI-native tools alike, monetization sits entirely on a subscription / one-time-purchase / enterprise-contract / open-core / donation / bundled-into-a-larger-paid-product spectrum. This is a meaningful structural fact for anyone benchmarking a notes product against "how do similar apps make money" — the honest answer across this entire category is "not ads."

### Growth & acquisition model

Organic/freemium conversion is by far the most common acquisition model (51% of the market) — consistent with the near-total absence of paid-advertising-led growth strategies in this category.

## Data Quality Note

Most financial figures for privately held companies in this report are **third-party estimates** (from aggregators such as Crunchbase, PitchBook, Tracxn, and Latka/GetLatka) or **self-reported figures from press interviews and company blog posts** — not audited financial statements. Multiple companies show materially conflicting figures across sources (e.g., Basecamp's revenue estimates range from ~$35M to ~$280M depending on source; Roam Research's total funding is cited anywhere from ~$9M to ~$40M). Every profile flags disclosed vs. estimated figures individually, and this matrix preserves those distinctions where space allows — treat all non-public-company figures here as **directional, not precise**, and verify directly with primary sources (SEC filings for public companies, or the company itself) before using any number for a decision with real stakes.

**CAC, LTV, and LTV:CAC figures are a separate and even softer category of estimate.** No company in this dataset publicly discloses actual customer acquisition cost or lifetime value. Every figure in the Monetization & Unit Economics section above was modeled from the company's business-model archetype (self-serve consumer freemium vs. enterprise sales-led vs. bundled-feature vs. open-source, etc.) combined with its disclosed or estimated pricing — the same method an outside analyst would use with no access to the company's actual books. Treat these figures as illustrative of *relative* patterns across business-model archetypes (e.g., "enterprise sales-led costs more to acquire but is worth far more per customer than freemium consumer") rather than as point estimates for any single company.
