# Capacities

## Overview
- Privately held, self-funded/bootstrapped company (Capacities Labs GmbH); explicitly states it funds itself through subscriptions rather than venture capital, though it has received some non-dilutive/innovation-program support.
- Founded 2022; HQ in Stuttgart, Germany.
- Employee count estimate: small independent team, likely under 10-15 people (estimated based on the company's self-described "independent team" framing and bootstrapped funding model, not a disclosed figure).

## Business Model
- Freemium subscription SaaS, direct-to-consumer, sold via the Capacities website and mobile app stores.
- Pricing (per capacities.io/pricing): Basic — free (unlimited spaces/objects/blocks, cross-device sync, full-text search, offline support, up to 5GB media uploads); Pro — $9.99/month or $119.88/year (adds task management, AI assistant, calendar integrations, smart queries, unlimited media uploads), marketed as "Most Popular"; Believer — from $12.49/month or $149.88/year (all Pro features plus beta access to major new features, positioned as direct support for the independent team). An optional "Plus AI Power-Up" add-on is available at $8.33/month (billed annually) for a larger AI usage budget.
- Target customers: PKM/"tools for thought" enthusiasts and knowledge workers who want an object-based, structured note-taking system — similar audience to Tana/Notion users but with a stronger emphasis on a distinctive "content-type" (object-based) organizational model.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: an object-type-based information model (notes are typed objects — e.g., "Person," "Book," "Project" — with structured properties) rather than a flat page/block hierarchy, aiming to combine the flexibility of freeform notes with database-like structure without requiring users to build their own schemas from scratch (unlike Notion or Tana's more manual setup).
- Platform availability: web, desktop (Mac/Windows), and mobile (iOS/Android per App Store listing), with cross-device sync included even in the free tier.
- Key features: built-in object types with structured properties, smart/dynamic queries across the knowledge base, calendar and task integration, an AI assistant (paid tiers), and a visual "mind map"/canvas view — marketed under the tagline "a studio for your mind."
- Company positioning: transparent, community-funded independent development model, similar in spirit to Logseq/Obsidian's community trust-building but with a fully proprietary, closed-source, subscription-funded (not open-source) product.

## Competitive Position
Capacities differentiates through its object-based information model, offering more built-in structure than Roam/Logseq's pure outliner approach while being more approachable/less setup-heavy than Notion or Tana's schema-building. Its bootstrapped, VC-free model builds trust with users wary of PKM tools that pivot or shut down after running out of venture funding (a real anxiety in this market following past tool sunsets), but the tradeoff is slower feature velocity and a much smaller marketing budget than VC-backed rivals like Tana or Anytype. It's a credible niche player for users who specifically want the object-type paradigm, but lacks the funding to compete broadly on AI feature parity.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user freemium subscription — Pro tier at $9.99/month ($119.88/year), marketed as "Most Popular."
- **Secondary monetization:** "Believer" superfan tier (from $12.49/month or $149.88/year) that bundles beta access and functions as a direct-support upsell for loyal users, plus an optional "Plus AI Power-Up" add-on ($8.33/month) for expanded AI usage budget; no ads, enterprise tier, or data licensing — consistent with its explicitly self-funded, subscription-only model.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — a generous free Basic tier (unlimited spaces/objects, cross-device sync) drives App Store/website discovery and word-of-mouth within PKM/"tools for thought" communities, with no VC-funded paid-acquisition spend given its bootstrapped positioning.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $5–$25 (estimated) — at the low end of the freemium consumer-app band, reflecting a small bootstrapped team relying almost entirely on organic App Store discovery and community trust-building rather than any paid marketing budget.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $150–$350 (estimated), based on blended Pro/Believer pricing (~$10–12.50/month) retained for roughly 2–3 years among a self-selected, loyalty-motivated user base (the "Believer" tier itself signals above-average retention intent).
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~16.7:1 (estimated) ($250 mid LTV / $15 mid CAC) — very healthy, typical of a lean bootstrapped subscription business with near-zero acquisition spend and a loyal, community-funded user base.
- **User demographics:** PKM/"tools for thought" enthusiasts and knowledge workers seeking structured, object-based note-taking, roughly 25–40, similar audience profile to Tana/Notion power users; skews toward German/European early base given Stuttgart HQ, with a broader international community typical of PKM software niches. Inferred from product positioning and company's "independent team"/community-funded framing rather than disclosed demographic data.
- **Estimated user geography:** Europe-centric with US/global PKM community reach, top countries: Germany (~25%), United States (~25%), United Kingdom (~8%), Canada (~5%) — estimated from Stuttgart HQ, bootstrapped German team, and Tana/Notion-adjacent international "tools for thought" audience, not disclosed.
