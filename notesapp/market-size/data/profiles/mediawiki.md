# MediaWiki (Wikimedia Foundation)

## Overview
- Open-source software (GPL) developed and stewarded by the Wikimedia Foundation, a 501(c)(3) nonprofit — not a commercial company. MediaWiki also has an independent open-source community/user base beyond Wikimedia itself (e.g., Fandom/Wikia, corporate intranets).
- Wikimedia Foundation founded in 2003; MediaWiki software originates from Wikipedia's own platform, first developed 2001–2002. HQ: San Francisco, California.
- Employee count: approximately 650 (Wikimedia Foundation, 2025).

## Business Model
- Free, open-source software; the Wikimedia Foundation itself is funded almost entirely by individual donations (small-dollar donor campaigns) plus a smaller commercial revenue stream.
- No direct "pricing" for MediaWiki software — it's free to download and self-host. The Foundation's own revenue comes from donations ($184M in contributions in FY2025) and Wikimedia Enterprise, a paid API product for large-scale commercial reusers (e.g., search engines, AI companies) that generated $8.3M in FY2025 (+148% YoY), about 4% of total revenue.
- Target "customer" for MediaWiki software: any organization wanting a free, scalable, battle-tested wiki engine (Wikipedia-proven at massive scale); target customer for Wikimedia Enterprise: large commercial consumers of Wikipedia data (search engines, LLM/AI companies).

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: MediaWiki is the software that powers Wikipedia, proven at web-scale (billions of pageviews), with a mature extension ecosystem (Semantic MediaWiki, VisualEditor, Cargo, etc.) and deep versioning/revision-history capabilities.
- Platform availability: self-hosted (PHP/MySQL); also available via third-party hosts and platforms like Fandom/Wikia (commercial, ad-supported) and Miraheze (free community hosting).
- Key features: robust revision history and diffing, massive extension library, multilingual support, category/namespace systems, strong API for programmatic access, proven scalability.

## Financial Profile
- Revenue: Wikimedia Foundation total revenue $208.6M (FY2025); expenses $190.9M; endowment $169.4M. MediaWiki software itself generates no direct revenue — it's a cost center/mission deliverable of the Foundation.
- Adoption: MediaWiki powers Wikipedia (one of the top-10 most-visited sites globally) and all Wikimedia projects, plus thousands of independent wikis worldwide (corporate intranets, fan wikis via Fandom, community projects via Miraheze); exact independent-install counts are not centrally tracked.
- Funding: nonprofit, donation-funded — not a for-profit entity, no equity funding or valuation applicable. Wikimedia Enterprise is the notable commercial revenue line, monetizing AI/search-engine consumption of Wikipedia data.
- Growth trajectory: donation revenue has grown steadily year over year; Wikimedia Enterprise is the fastest-growing segment (+148% YoY in FY2025), reflecting rising demand for licensed, structured Wikipedia data from AI companies.

## Competitive Position
MediaWiki has no real commercial "competitor" in its core mission (nonprofit knowledge infrastructure) but competes indirectly with Confluence, Notion, and other wiki/knowledge tools when evaluated purely as software for internal documentation. Its moat is unmatched scale-proven reliability (having run Wikipedia for two decades) and zero licensing cost, but it lacks modern UX polish and enterprise support compared to commercial wiki products, and the Foundation's own financial sustainability depends on continued public donations plus the newer Wikimedia Enterprise data-licensing business.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Donations (individual small-dollar donor campaigns funding the Wikimedia Foundation, which stewards MediaWiki); the software itself is free/open-source with no direct pricing.
- **Secondary monetization:** Wikimedia Enterprise — a paid API product for large-scale commercial reusers (search engines, AI/LLM companies), generating $8.3M in FY2025 (~4% of total revenue).
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Donation-funded — no sales motion for the software itself; Wikipedia's scale and reputation drive organic adoption of MediaWiki by third parties (corporate intranets, Fandom/Wikia, Miraheze), while Wikimedia Enterprise uses a small dedicated commercial team to sign large data-reuser contracts.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ~$0 commercial CAC for MediaWiki software itself (nonprofit, donation-funded, no acquisition spend against software adoption); donor-acquisition cost (a distinct fundraising metric) is not disclosed and out of scope here.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $0 for the free software; Wikimedia Enterprise customer LTV is undisclosed but likely substantial per-account given the $8.3M revenue concentrated among search engines and AI companies (assumed six-to-seven-figure annual contracts per customer).
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** N/A — nonprofit mission-driven software distribution, not a commercial CAC/LTV model.
- **User demographics:** Extremely broad — from individual hobbyist wiki operators to large enterprises (via Fandom/Wikia, Miraheze, corporate intranets) and, for Wikimedia Enterprise, large commercial AI/search companies; geography is global, mirroring Wikipedia's worldwide reach.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, top countries: United States (~15%), India (~10%), Germany (~7%), Brazil (~5%), Japan (~5%) — estimated from Wikipedia/Wikimedia disclosed pageview and readership patterns reflecting worldwide internet population, not disclosed.
