# Evernote

## Overview
- Parent company: Bending Spoons S.p.A., a Milan-based Italian technology holding company that acquired Evernote Corporation in January 2023 (deal announced November 2022). Bending Spoons is itself publicly traded (filed for a U.S. IPO in mid-2026, per SEC filings).
- Founded / launched: Evernote was founded in 2004 (public launch 2008) by Stepan Pachikov; later led by Phil Libin as CEO during its growth years. Originally HQ'd in Redwood City, California; operations have been restructured under Bending Spoons' Milan-based leadership since the 2023 acquisition.
- Employee count: Evernote's own headcount was sharply reduced post-acquisition (widely reported layoffs cut the workforce from roughly 300+ to a small remaining team, with much of engineering relocated/absorbed into Bending Spoons); Bending Spoons overall employs roughly 700+ people across its portfolio of apps (AOL, Vimeo, WeTransfer, Evernote, Meetup, and others).

## Business Model
- Freemium subscription: a limited free tier plus paid personal and business subscription tiers, sold direct-to-consumer and to teams/enterprises.
- Pricing (as of 2026, post-restructure): free plan capped at 50 notes and one notebook; paid tiers restructured in late 2025 into "Starter" at $99.99/year and "Advanced" at $249.99/year, with a custom-priced Enterprise plan. This followed a 2023 price hike that raised the former Personal plan from about $69.99/year to $129.99/year (~86% increase) shortly after the Bending Spoons acquisition.
- Target customer: prosumers and small-to-mid-size teams/businesses who need robust search, web clipping, and document/PDF archiving; increasingly monetized harder per-user than in its earlier growth-stage years.

## Product & Positioning
- Differentiators: powerful full-text and handwriting/image search (including searchable PDFs and photos of handwriting), the Web Clipper browser extension, and long-standing brand recognition as one of the original "second brain" note apps.
- Platform availability: Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, and web.
- Key features: notebooks and tags, document scanning, task management, calendar integration, and AI-assisted search/summarization features added under Bending Spoons ownership as part of its "AI Notes" modernization push.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: Evernote-specific revenue is not separately broken out, but it is one of Bending Spoons' "main businesses." Bending Spoons' consolidated revenue grew 95% year-over-year to $1.31 billion in 2025, and more than doubled to $601.3 million in Q1 2026 alone (company SEC IPO filings); Evernote's individual contribution within that portfolio is not disclosed.
- Users: Bending Spoons' overall app portfolio served over 500 million monthly active users and more than 9 million monthly paying customers as of March 2026 (across all its apps, not Evernote alone). Evernote's standalone active-user count is not currently disclosed, though it was reported in the tens of millions at its historical peak (mid-2010s).
- Funding history: Evernote raised roughly $290 million in VC funding over its independent life (investors including Sequoia Capital, Morgenthaler Ventures, DFJ Growth, and CBC Capital), reaching a peak valuation around $1 billion (2012). It was acquired by Bending Spoons in a deal valued at roughly $10 million in enterprise value plus debt (widely reported as a distressed-asset sale reflecting the company's decline from its earlier billion-dollar valuation).
- Growth trajectory: net revenue retention for Evernote averaged 99% from Q1 2023 through Q1 2026 under Bending Spoons (per IPO filings), suggesting stabilization after the price increases, though this reflects retention of a smaller, higher-paying customer base rather than net user growth.

## Competitive Position
- Evernote is the cautionary tale of the category: an early category-defining leader that lost ground to Notion, OneNote, and Apple/Google's free bundled apps during the mid-2010s, then was acquired as a turnaround/harvest asset by a roll-up operator (Bending Spoons) known for cutting costs and raising prices on legacy apps.
- Its remaining moat is switching costs for long-time power users with large archives (years of notes, clipped web pages, scanned documents) who find migration costly, plus brand recognition — but it has largely ceded new-user acquisition to free alternatives.
- Its trajectory now depends on whether Bending Spoons' price-increase-plus-AI-features strategy can sustain a smaller, more profitable subscriber base rather than on regaining broad market share.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user subscription (Starter/Advanced consumer tiers at $99.99–$249.99/year) sold direct-to-consumer.
- **Secondary monetization:** Custom-priced Enterprise tier for teams/businesses; recent price hikes (2023, ~86% increase) functioning as a de facto monetization lever on the existing base rather than new revenue streams; no ads, no data licensing disclosed.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — a capped free tier (50 notes/1 notebook) funnels into paid plans via App Store/organic search and long-standing brand recognition, supplemented by Bending Spoons' portfolio cross-promotion; some enterprise sales-assist for the Enterprise tier.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $40–$120 (estimated) — a paid-ads/organic-mixed consumer subscription with a recognizable legacy brand but a mature, no-longer-growing user base, consistent with the paid-ads-driven consumer subscription archetype at the lower-middle of its typical band.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $300–$500 (estimated) — e.g., ~$100–$150/year Starter/Advanced pricing × ~3-year average retention (supported by disclosed ~99% net revenue retention under Bending Spoons) ≈ $300–$450.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4:1 (estimated) — healthy (>3:1), consistent with Bending Spoons' playbook of raising prices and retaining a smaller, more profitable subscriber base rather than chasing new-user growth.
- **User demographics:** Prosumers and small business/team users, skewing 25–44, knowledge workers and small-business owners who value archiving/search depth; long-tenured power users with large legacy note archives. Partly disclosed via Bending Spoons' portfolio-wide MAU/paying-customer figures; occupation/age mix inferred from positioning.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America/Global, with a notably large legacy Japan user base, top countries: United States (~35%), Japan (~12%), United Kingdom (~7%), Germany (~5%) — estimated from original US HQ (Redwood City), long-standing brand strength in Japan, and Bending Spoons' EU-based operations, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
