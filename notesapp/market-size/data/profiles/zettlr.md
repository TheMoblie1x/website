# Zettlr

## Overview
- Free and open-source software (FOSS); not a company in the traditional sense — no ownership/VC structure, run as a community/donation-funded project.
- Founded 2017 as a personal passion project by Hendrik Erz, a researcher dissatisfied with existing Markdown/academic writing tools. Origin: Germany/academic research community (Erz is an academic researcher).
- "Employee count": no employees; maintained by Hendrik Erz along with a small group of volunteer/community maintainers and contributors.

## Business Model
- No traditional commercial pricing — Zettlr is entirely free to download and use, licensed as open-source software (available via GitHub).
- Revenue model is donation-based: users can support development via Patreon (recurring) or one-time PayPal tips; there are no paid tiers, subscriptions, or enterprise plans.
- Target customer: academics, researchers, PhD students, and technical/long-form writers who need a Markdown-native tool built specifically for scholarly writing workflows (citations, footnotes, bibliography integration) rather than general note-taking.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: purpose-built for academic and long-form publishing workflows — deep Markdown support, citation management (Zotero/CSL integration), footnotes, and export to formats like LaTeX/Word via Pandoc, positioned as a "one-stop publication workbench."
- Platform availability: Desktop only — Windows, macOS, and Linux (built with Electron); no official mobile app or hosted web version.
- Key features: Zettelkasten-style linking, live Markdown preview, citation/bibliography integration, multi-file project management, and Pandoc-based export to academic and publishing formats (LaTeX, Word, PDF, etc.).

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: not applicable in a traditional sense; donation income is not publicly disclosed but is almost certainly modest (typical of small-scale, single-maintainer-led open-source projects) — estimated to be in the low tens-of-thousands-of-dollars-per-year range at most, based on comparable donation-funded FOSS projects.
- Users/customers: not officially disclosed, but the project states it has spread to users on all continents and at prestigious universities worldwide by 2020, with continued adoption growth through major version releases (2.0 in 2021, 3.0 in 2023).
- Funding history: no venture funding — sustained purely by community donations (Patreon/PayPal) and volunteer contribution; broader open-source funding infrastructure (e.g., EU Sovereign Tech Fund-style programs) represents a potential but unconfirmed funding avenue rather than a current source.
- Growth trajectory: steady, organic growth driven by academic word-of-mouth and continued open-source development; growth is constrained by its single-niche (academic writing), donation-only funding model rather than any evidence of decline.

## Competitive Position
- Zettlr occupies a specific, defensible niche as the leading open-source, Markdown-native tool purpose-built for academic writing and citation-heavy publishing workflows, distinct from general PKM tools like Obsidian or Notion.
- Its moat is community trust, purpose-fit for scholarly workflows, and zero-cost/open-source status — appealing to academics wary of vendor lock-in or subscription costs — but it lacks the venture funding, AI feature velocity, and broader ecosystem (plugins, integrations) of well-funded commercial competitors like Obsidian.
- Best understood as a stable, mission-driven open-source tool rather than a growth business; its long-term sustainability depends on continued volunteer/maintainer commitment rather than financial scaling.
