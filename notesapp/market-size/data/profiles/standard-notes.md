# Standard Notes

## Overview
- Acquired subsidiary: acquired by Proton AG (maker of Proton Mail/Proton VPN) in April 2024; deal terms not disclosed. Prior to that, Standard Notes was bootstrapped and self-funded.
- Founded by Mo Bitar; originally an independent, self-funded, privacy-focused note app maker. Mo Bitar subsequently left Proton after the acquisition (reported in privacy-community forums in 2025).
- Employee count estimate: not separately disclosed; now operates within Proton AG's broader employee base (Proton AG has several hundred employees company-wide).

## Business Model
- Freemium subscription model: free tier with core encrypted note-taking; paid tier unlocks extended features (extensions, more editors, longer history, larger file storage).
- Pricing: premium plan listed around $90/year standard (frequently discounted to roughly $58.50/year during promotions), per third-party review aggregators; now likely bundled/cross-sold with Proton's broader subscription bundles (Proton Unlimited) post-acquisition.
- Target customer: privacy-conscious consumers and prosumers, historically security/privacy community members; post-acquisition, increasingly cross-sold to Proton's existing privacy-focused user base.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: end-to-end encrypted, open-source, minimalist note-taking with an emphasis on longevity and plain-text portability (notes stored in simple, exportable formats to avoid lock-in).
- Platform availability: Web, Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android.
- Key features: end-to-end encryption by default, open-source clients and server, extensible editor system (Markdown, code, rich text, spreadsheets via extensions), and now integration potential with the wider Proton privacy suite (Mail, Calendar, Drive, VPN, Pass).

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: not disclosed pre- or post-acquisition; pre-acquisition described as self-funded through subscription revenue only (no VC funding taken).
- Acquisition price: undisclosed (TechCrunch, Tech.eu, and TechRadar all confirm the deal but not financial terms).
- Users: not publicly disclosed; historically described as a smaller player relative to Notion/Obsidian, with a loyal privacy-focused niche user base.
- Growth trajectory: post-acquisition financials are now consolidated into Proton AG's private reporting, reducing standalone visibility going forward.

## Competitive Position
Standard Notes competes in the encrypted/privacy-first note-taking niche alongside Joplin and Notesnook, but its acquisition by Proton gives it a distribution advantage (cross-sell into Proton's large privacy-conscious user base) that its independent peers lack. Its moat going forward is less about independent brand strength and more about being a component of Proton's broader "private alternative to Google/Microsoft" ecosystem play.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user subscription (freemium — premium plan ~$90/year, frequently discounted).
- **Secondary monetization:** Cross-sell/bundling into Proton Unlimited (Mail, VPN, Drive, Calendar, Pass) post-acquisition; extension/editor add-ons.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion, now augmented by distribution into Proton's large existing privacy-conscious install base (content/SEO in the privacy community, plus Proton's cross-promotion channels); historically less than a quarter of Standard Notes users were already Proton users, so cross-sell upside remains largely untapped.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $15–$60 (estimated). Indie/privacy-freemium organic archetype, now trending toward the higher end as Proton applies some paid-channel and cross-promotion spend post-acquisition.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $200–$450 (estimated) — e.g., ~$90/yr list price (often discounted to ~$60) × ~3–4-year avg retention (privacy-focused users tend to be sticky) ≈ $270–$360, with further uplift potential from Proton Unlimited bundle attach.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~7:1 (estimated) — healthy, and likely to improve further as Proton's distribution lowers effective CAC over time.
- **User demographics:** Privacy- and security-conscious consumers/prosumers, historically overlapping with the broader digital-privacy/security community; 300,000+ reported users; roughly 25–45, skewing toward Europe (Proton's Swiss/EU base) and tech-savvy geographies; no granular age/gender data disclosed.

- **Estimated user geography:** Europe-heavy, top countries: Germany (~15%), United States (~15%), France (~9%), Switzerland (~7%), United Kingdom (~7%) — estimated from investor base/parent-company HQ (Proton, Geneva, Switzerland) and privacy-community concentration in Europe, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
