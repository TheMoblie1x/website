# BookStack (open-source)

## Overview
- Open-source community project (MIT licensed), not a company. Maintained primarily by creator Dan Brown and ~186+ community contributors; source repository migrated from GitHub to Codeberg (still mirrored/discoverable on GitHub).
- No formal corporate parent; began as a personal/community project. Marked its 10th anniversary in July 2025, implying a founding around 2015.
- "Employee count": not applicable — run by a lead maintainer plus volunteer contributors; no disclosed paid staff. Revenue (if any) would come from optional donations/sponsorship, not a company payroll.

## Business Model
- Free and open-source (MIT license); self-hosted software with no official paid tier from the project itself.
- Monetization is indirect: third-party managed-hosting providers (e.g., various VPS/app-hosting marketplaces) sell hosted BookStack instances; the core project itself is sustained via GitHub Sponsors/donations rather than product revenue.
- Target customer: technical teams, IT departments, and individuals who want a self-hosted, low-cost internal wiki/documentation tool and are comfortable with self-hosting (PHP/Laravel stack, MySQL).

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: simple, intuitive "book/chapter/page" content hierarchy that's easier to grasp for non-technical users than flat wiki page trees (a key contrast with MediaWiki/DokuWiki).
- Platform availability: self-hosted (Docker, bare-metal LAMP-style installs); web-based UI only, no official SaaS/cloud offering from the core project.
- Key features: WYSIWYG + Markdown editing, granular role-based permissions, built-in search, diagram integration (draw.io), API access, multi-authentication support (LDAP, SAML, OIDC, social login).

## Financial Profile (estimated)
- Revenue: no company revenue — open-source project; any funds are donation/sponsorship-based and not publicly quantified.
- Adoption: 16,926 GitHub stars and 186 direct contributors as of its July 2025 10th-anniversary post, making it the most-starred PHP-based wiki on GitHub. Actual install base (self-hosted, uncounted by nature) is unknown but widely cited as popular among self-hosting/homelab and small-to-mid IT teams.
- Funding: none — community/volunteer-driven; no VC or institutional backing.
- Growth trajectory: steady, long-running community project (10+ years active development, regular releases) rather than a fast-scaling commercial product.

## Competitive Position
BookStack sits in the self-hosted/open-source documentation-wiki niche alongside Wiki.js, DokuWiki, Outline (open-core), and Docmost, competing on simplicity and ease of setup rather than enterprise features or scale. Its moat is purely community goodwill and its distinctive, easy-to-use content model — it has no commercial backing, no cloud offering, and no revenue engine, making it durable as a free tool but structurally unable to compete with funded platforms (Confluence, Notion, GitHub Wiki) on enterprise features, support SLAs, or integrations.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Donations (free open-source software; no official paid tier from the project itself).
- **Secondary monetization:** Third-party managed-hosting providers reselling hosted BookStack instances (not captured by the project); GitHub Sponsors/donation income to the maintainer.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Donation-funded — the project has no formal sales or marketing motion; growth comes from GitHub/Codeberg visibility, self-hosting/homelab community word-of-mouth, and inclusion in app-hosting marketplaces (e.g., one-click installers).
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ~$0 for the free self-hosted userbase (no acquisition spend, purely organic/community); $500–$1,500 (estimated) framing for a third-party host or IT team's effective "cost" to evaluate/deploy and support it internally, since there's no vendor sales motion to fund.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $0 direct to the project (no subscription revenue captured); indirect/ecosystem value only via donations, which are immaterial and unquantified.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** N/A — not a commercial product; donation-supported open source with no per-user unit economics.
- **User demographics:** Technical teams, IT departments, and self-hosting/homelab individuals comfortable running a PHP/Laravel + MySQL stack; skews technical, cost-sensitive, small-to-mid organizations.
- **Estimated user geography:** Europe-led with a strong US self-hosting/homelab contingent, top countries: United Kingdom (~20%), United States (~30%), Germany (~12%) — estimated from creator/maintainer base location (UK) and self-hosting/homelab community concentration, not disclosed.
