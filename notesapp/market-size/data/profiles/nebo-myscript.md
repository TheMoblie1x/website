# Nebo (MyScript)

## Overview
- Parent company: MyScript SAS — privately held, independent French software company (not VC-mega-funded; investor base is smaller/regional).
- Founded: MyScript founded 1998; Nebo app launched 2016 built on MyScript's Interactive Ink handwriting-recognition technology.
- HQ: Nantes, France.
- Employees: Approximately 96 total employees at MyScript (per Latka/company data, 2024 reporting).

## Business Model
- Nebo: freemium/one-time-purchase and subscription hybrid depending on platform (varies by app store — iOS/Android historically offered free-with-trial plus a one-time unlock or subscription for full feature set; Windows Store version has had different pricing history). Exact current SKU-level pricing varies by platform and was not fully itemized in this research pass.
- MyScript's primary revenue model is B2B: licensing its Interactive Ink handwriting-recognition SDK to OEMs (device makers, software platforms) in addition to direct-to-consumer Nebo app sales — meaning Nebo is partly a showcase/consumer product for the underlying licensed technology.
- Target customer: Nebo targets students and professionals wanting real-time handwriting-to-text conversion on stylus devices (Windows Ink, iPad, Android tablets); the broader MyScript business targets device OEMs and enterprise software vendors.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: best-in-class real-time handwriting recognition (converts handwriting to typed text/math/diagrams as you write) via the proprietary Interactive Ink engine — this is MyScript's core IP moat, developed over 25+ years.
- Platforms: iOS/iPadOS, Android, Windows (broad cross-platform coverage, notably including Windows/Surface devices where Goodnotes/Notability have weaker presence).
- Key features: live handwriting-to-text conversion, math equation recognition and solving, diagram/shape recognition, and the ability to keep content as "digital ink" while still being searchable/convertible — a fundamentally different technical approach than the raster-ink-storage model used by most competitors.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed)
- Revenue: MyScript reported approximately $18 million in revenue as of the most recent reporting cited (Latka, 2024), up from about $10.4 million in 2021 — showing steady growth over that period.
- Users: No official Nebo-specific user counts found; MyScript's SDK is embedded in numerous third-party devices/apps, giving it indirect reach beyond direct Nebo downloads.
- Funding: Investor base reported to include Atlanpole, DoubleDay Holdings, Irus Partenaires, Myrian Capital, and XAnge; total disclosed funding figures in public trackers are inconsistent/likely incomplete (one source cites just $2.7M total funding, which likely undercounts given the company's 25+ year history and revenue scale) — treat total funding figures as unreliable/estimated.
- Growth trajectory: Revenue growth from ~$10.4M (2021) to ~$18M (recent) suggests healthy, steady expansion, likely driven by both OEM licensing deals and direct Nebo subscription growth.

## Competitive Position
MyScript/Nebo holds a genuine technical moat — its handwriting-recognition engine is widely regarded as superior in accuracy to competitors' built-in OCR, and its long history of OEM licensing gives it a B2B revenue base that consumer-only apps lack. However, Nebo itself has less brand recognition among students than Goodnotes/Notability, and its UX/ecosystem (templates, community) is less developed, positioning it as a technology-differentiated niche player rather than the category's growth leader.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** B2B SDK/technology licensing — MyScript licenses its Interactive Ink handwriting-recognition engine to device OEMs and software platforms; this is the company's stated primary revenue driver, with Nebo functioning partly as a consumer showcase for the underlying tech.
- **Secondary monetization:** Direct-to-consumer Nebo app sales (hybrid one-time-purchase/subscription, varies by platform).
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Sales-led enterprise — MyScript's core SDK-licensing business is acquired via a direct B2B sales motion targeting device makers and enterprise software vendors, while the consumer Nebo app relies on separate organic app-store acquisition as a secondary channel.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $10,000–$25,000 (estimated) per enterprise/OEM licensing client. Reflects a deal-based B2B SDK licensing archetype with long sales cycles involving direct enterprise sales and technical integration support; consumer Nebo CAC is far lower (likely $5–$30, organic-app-store-driven) but is not the company's primary revenue engine.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $50,000–$150,000 (estimated) per OEM/enterprise licensing relationship, based on multi-year embedded SDK contracts (device makers rarely re-litigate a handwriting-recognition engine choice once shipped) generating recurring or royalty-based licensing revenue over 3–7+ years.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~5.7:1 (estimated). Healthy for a sales-led B2B licensing business, consistent with high switching costs once an OEM has integrated the SDK.
- **User demographics:** Primary buyers are device OEMs, PC/tablet makers, and enterprise software vendors (B2B, technical decision-makers); secondary/end-user demographic (Nebo app itself) is students and professionals using stylus-enabled tablets/laptops (notably Windows/Surface users), skewing toward technical/productivity-focused adults.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed (B2B licensing), top countries: United States (~25%), France (~12%), South Korea (~10%) — estimated from HQ location (Nantes, France) / platform reach (global OEM/enterprise SDK licensing to device makers worldwide, e.g. Samsung, Lenovo, Microsoft), not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
