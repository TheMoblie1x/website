# Monday.com

## Overview
- Public: monday.com Ltd. (NASDAQ: MNDY).
- Founded in 2012 (as dapulse) by Roy Mann and Eran Zinman, rebranded to monday.com in 2017; HQ in Tel Aviv, Israel.
- Employee count is estimated in the range of 2,500-3,000 as of recent filings, per company disclosures and third-party trackers.

## Business Model
- Per-seat SaaS subscription model built around a flexible "Work OS" (customizable boards/workflows), with dedicated product lines for monday Work Management, monday CRM, monday Dev, and monday Service.
- Published pricing tiers (2025-2026, billed per seat with minimum-seat requirements): Free, Basic (~$9/seat/month), Standard (~$12/seat/month), Pro (~$19/seat/month), and custom-priced Enterprise, billed annually with volume-based seat packs.
- Target customer ranges from SMB through enterprise, with an increasing enterprise push reflected in the growth of large ($100k+ ARR) customer accounts, and specific product editions (CRM, Dev, Service) targeting departmental buyers beyond generic project management.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator is a highly visual, no-code customizable "board" data model that can be configured into project trackers, CRMs, dev sprints, or service desks without custom development — docs/notes functionality exists as a secondary "monday Docs" feature embedded within the broader Work OS rather than a standalone product.
- Available via web, desktop (Mac/Windows), and mobile (iOS/Android) apps, with a large marketplace of integrations and automations.
- Positioned as a horizontal, multi-product Work OS platform competing with Asana and ClickUp, increasingly emphasizing its CRM product line (monday CRM crossed $100 million in ARR) as a growth vector alongside core project/work management.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Disclosed Q2 2025 revenue of $299.0 million, up 27% year-over-year (audited, company-reported figure); full-year 2025 revenue guidance issued at $1.224-1.229 billion, representing approximately 26% year-over-year growth — among the fastest growth rates of any public company in this comparison set.
- Non-GAAP operating margin was reported at 15% in the same period, and the company disclosed a record number of net-new customers exceeding $100k in ARR, alongside monday CRM independently crossing $100 million in ARR.
- Publicly traded (IPO in 2021); market cap was approximately $3.4-3.7 billion as of September 2026, having declined significantly (roughly -70%+) from prior-year highs amid broader concerns about AI disruption to seat-based SaaS pricing models, despite continued strong revenue growth.

## Competitive Position
- monday.com is a fast-growing, multi-product Work OS platform with among the strongest revenue growth rates in this peer set (mid-20s% YoY), driven by successful expansion beyond core project management into CRM and dev/service verticals.
- Its moat is the flexibility and approachability of its visual board-based data model, which lowers the barrier for non-technical teams to build custom workflows, plus increasing multi-product stickiness (Work Management + CRM + Dev + Service) that raises switching costs.
- Despite strong fundamentals, the market has applied a steep valuation discount reflecting fears that AI agents and seat-based pricing models generally face disruption risk — a headwind shared with Asana and ClickUp but reflected most sharply in monday.com's 2025-2026 stock performance.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-seat SaaS subscription — Basic, Standard, Pro, and Enterprise tiers priced per seat/month in fixed seat-block increments (e.g., 3, 5, 10 seats), across the core Work Management product.
- **Secondary monetization:** Multi-product expansion (monday CRM, monday Dev, monday Service), AI add-ons/credits, and Enterprise-tier admin/security features driving upsell within existing accounts.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG) — monday.com's signature motion is a highly viral, template-driven self-serve funnel converting free trials into paid seat-block subscriptions, complemented by inside/field sales for larger multi-product enterprise deals.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $1,000–$3,000 (estimated) — public-company scale PLG+sales blend; monday.com has historically run high sales & marketing spend (~40-45% of revenue) to sustain its above-peer growth rate.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $5,000–$15,000 (estimated) — reflecting strong multi-product net-dollar retention and expansion (CRM/Dev/Service cross-sell) on top of Work Management seats, with average customer lifetime of ~4-5 years.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4.5:1 (estimated).
- **User demographics:** SMB to mid-market teams initially, expanding upmarket into enterprise; broad functional spread (marketing, ops, sales, dev) given the flexible board-based model; strong presence in Europe/Israel-origin base alongside North America.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, top countries: United States (~50%), United Kingdom (~10%), Israel (~5%), Australia (~5%), Germany (~5%) — estimated from HQ location (Tel Aviv/NYC) and 10-K geographic revenue disclosure (~50% US / ~50% international, EMEA-heavy), not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
