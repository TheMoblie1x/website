# Quip (Salesforce)

## Overview
- Public parent: Salesforce, Inc. (NYSE: CRM). Quip was acquired by Salesforce in 2016 (deal valued at roughly $750 million-$1 billion at the time) and now operates as a Salesforce-owned productivity product rather than an independent company.
- Quip was founded in 2012 by Bret Taylor (former Facebook CTO, later Salesforce co-CEO) and Kevin Gibbs; originally HQ'd in San Francisco/Redwood City, now folded into Salesforce's HQ in San Francisco, CA.
- Standalone Quip employee count is not disclosed; it operates as a product line within Salesforce, which employs roughly 76,000+ people company-wide as of FY2025.

## Business Model
- Sold as a per-user subscription collaboration suite (docs, spreadsheets, chat) that can be purchased standalone or bundled with Salesforce Sales/Service Cloud via the "Quip Lightning" document component embedded directly into Salesforce records.
- Published pricing tiers historically range from roughly $10-$25/user/month for Starter/Standard editions up to enterprise pricing; some editions (e.g., Starter) cannot be combined with "Quip for Salesforce," per Salesforce's own pricing pages — reflecting Quip's dual life as both a standalone product and a Salesforce CRM add-on.
- Target customer is primarily existing Salesforce CRM customers (mid-market to enterprise sales/service organizations) who want live documents and spreadsheets embedded directly in CRM records, plus some standalone SMB/team adoption independent of Salesforce.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator is deep Salesforce CRM embedding: the "Lightning Document Component" lets users build and view live Quip docs/spreadsheets directly inside Sales Cloud and Service Cloud records (e.g., account plans embedded on an Opportunity page), a use case no generalist docs tool replicates natively.
- Available via web and native desktop/mobile apps (iOS, Android); includes built-in team chat alongside docs and spreadsheets.
- Positioned less as a broad docs competitor to Google Docs/Word and more as a CRM-adjacent collaboration layer; third-party live app development support has narrowed as of 2025, suggesting Salesforce has scaled back platform ambitions for Quip in favor of tighter CRM integration.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Quip has no standalone revenue disclosure; it is folded into Salesforce's overall subscription and support revenue. Salesforce's total FY2025 revenue was $37.9 billion, up 9% year-over-year, with operating income of $7.2 billion — Quip's specific contribution is immaterial to and not broken out within these figures.
- No disclosed standalone user/customer counts for Quip; adoption is generally tied to Salesforce's broader enterprise customer base (Salesforce serves hundreds of thousands of organizations globally).
- Acquisition price (2016) was reported at approximately $750 million-$1 billion; no subsequent independent funding or valuation events, as Quip has been wholly owned by Salesforce for nearly a decade. No distinct growth trajectory is disclosed — Quip is best characterized as a mature, slow-growth feature of the Salesforce platform rather than a growth product.

## Competitive Position
- Quip is a niche, CRM-embedded collaboration tool rather than a broad docs-market competitor; its relevance is almost entirely a function of Salesforce's CRM dominance rather than independent product momentum.
- Its moat is exclusive, deep integration into Salesforce records — a use case Google Docs, Notion, and Coda cannot match without heavy custom integration work — but this also caps its addressable market to Salesforce customers.
- Absent renewed investment, Quip functions as a retention/stickiness feature for Salesforce accounts rather than a standalone growth business, and has seen reduced platform ambition (e.g., pared-back third-party app support) in recent years.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Bundled into larger suite (free/add-on) — Quip is sold primarily as a per-user add-on to Salesforce Sales/Service Cloud licenses rather than as a standalone product.
- **Secondary monetization:** Standalone Quip Starter/Plus/Advanced plans (small residual direct-sale channel) and Quip's role driving broader Salesforce platform stickiness/upsell.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Sales-led enterprise — Quip is attached to existing Salesforce account relationships and sold through Salesforce's enterprise sales org rather than acquired independently.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ~$0–$200 standalone (estimated) — Quip rides Salesforce's existing enterprise sales motion as an add-on line item, so incremental CAC attributable to Quip alone is minimal.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $500–$1,500 (estimated) — modest standalone value given per-seat add-on pricing (~$10-25/user/mo) and typical multi-year Salesforce contract terms, though most value accrues to the parent CRM relationship.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** N/A — largely bundled feature (some residual standalone signal, roughly 3:1–5:1 estimated where directly sold).
- **User demographics:** Existing Salesforce CRM customers — enterprise sales/service teams needing embedded collaborative docs tied to CRM records, global but concentrated where Salesforce has deep account penetration.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, top countries: United States (~50%), United Kingdom (~10%), Australia (~7%), Germany (~6%), Japan (~5%) — estimated from HQ location (San Francisco) and Salesforce's global enterprise distribution, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
