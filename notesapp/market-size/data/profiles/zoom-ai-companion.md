# Zoom AI Companion (Zoom)

## Overview
- Bundled feature/product of Zoom Communications, Inc. (formerly Zoom Video Communications), a public company (NASDAQ: ZM). Not a standalone company.
- Zoom itself was founded in 2011 by Eric Yuan; headquartered in San Jose, California. AI Companion launched in 2023 as a generative-AI feature layered onto Zoom Workplace; version 3.0 (agentic capabilities) became generally available in 2026.
- Employee count: Zoom Communications overall reports roughly 8,400+ employees company-wide (parent-company figure; AI Companion has no separate headcount as it is a feature, not a separate business unit).

## Business Model
- Bundled-into-platform model: AI Companion Basic is included at no extra cost with eligible Zoom Workplace Basic, Pro, Business, and Enterprise plans; a more advanced AI Companion 3.0 tier is available to eligible Workplace users and also sold as a standalone subscription.
- Pricing: AI Companion (base) is free with paid Zoom licenses; standalone AI Companion access (without a full Zoom license) costs $10/user/month; AI Companion 2.0/advanced capabilities (custom AI assistants, document/knowledge-base integration, workflow automation) start at $13/user/month; a Custom AI add-on costs an additional $12/user/month. Source: eesel.ai/blog/zoom-ai, kiolo.com/en/blog/zoom-ai-companion-pricing.
- Target customer: the entire Zoom customer base — from individual/small-business Workplace subscribers to large enterprise accounts — since it is a platform-wide feature rather than a targeted product.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiators: deep native integration across the full Zoom Workplace suite (Meetings, Mail, Team Chat, Whiteboard, Zoom Docs), plus "agentic retrieval" in version 3.0 that can search across meeting summaries, transcripts, notes, and connected third-party apps like Google Drive and Microsoft OneDrive.
- Platform availability: built into Zoom's native apps (no separate bot/app required); works across Meetings, Chat, Mail, Whiteboard.
- Key features: meeting summaries, smart recordings with auto-generated chapters, real-time Q&A during calls, chat/email drafting assistance, and (in 3.0) agentic task completion ("conversation to completion") across connected apps.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- No standalone financials exist for AI Companion; it is a bundled feature of Zoom's broader subscription revenue, not separately reported.
- Parent context: Zoom Communications reported full-year revenue of approximately $4.67 billion for fiscal year 2025 (ended January 31, 2025), up ~3.1% year-over-year, with operating income of ~$813M and net income of ~$1.01B. Source: Nasdaq.com coverage of Zoom's FY2025 results, investors.zoom.us.
- User/customer counts: AI Companion usage is reported by Zoom in aggregate terms (e.g., "millions of accounts enabled") rather than as a standalone paying user base, since it is largely given away to drive retention of the core Zoom subscription.
- Growth trajectory: Zoom's overall revenue growth has slowed to low-single-digits as the core video-conferencing market matures; AI Companion is a strategic retention/differentiation lever (and incremental monetization via standalone/advanced tiers) rather than a new independent growth engine.

## Competitive Position
- As a free, deeply integrated feature bundled into an already-dominant video-conferencing platform, AI Companion poses a significant commoditization threat to standalone AI notetakers (tl;dv, Grain, Granola, Read AI, Avoma, Supernormal) that must justify a separate subscription for similar summarization functionality.
- Its moat is Zoom's massive installed base and platform integration (Mail, Chat, Whiteboard, Docs) rather than best-in-class AI quality; standalone competitors still differentiate on cross-platform support (many work across Zoom, Meet, and Teams simultaneously) and specialized features (sales coaching, cross-meeting analytics) that Zoom's bundled tool doesn't fully replicate.
- Zoom's strategy positions AI Companion primarily as a retention and platform-stickiness tool for its ~$4.7B core business rather than a standalone profit center, giving it a durable distribution advantage but a less specialized feature set than pure-play competitors.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Free/bundled into a larger paid platform — included at no extra cost with eligible Zoom Workplace Basic, Pro, Business, and Enterprise licenses.
- **Secondary monetization:** Standalone AI Companion subscription ($10/user/month) for non-Zoom-license users; AI Companion 2.0/advanced tier ($13/user/month) and a Custom AI add-on ($12/user/month) for existing Workplace customers.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Free/bundled (parent monetizes indirectly) — distributed automatically to Zoom's existing installed base as a retention and platform-stickiness lever rather than sold through a dedicated funnel.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ~$0 standalone (estimated) — AI Companion rides on Zoom's existing enterprise/SMB sales and marketing motion; no incremental acquisition spend is attributable to the feature itself.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $0 standalone (estimated) — value accrues to Zoom's core subscription LTV (retention/expansion uplift) rather than as a separately monetized product.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** N/A — bundled feature.
- **User demographics:** Entire Zoom Workplace customer base — individual/SMB subscribers through large enterprise accounts; global, with a skew toward North America and other mature Zoom markets.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, top countries: United States (~35%), United Kingdom (~8%), Japan (~7%), Germany (~6%) — estimated from HQ location / platform reach, not disclosed.
