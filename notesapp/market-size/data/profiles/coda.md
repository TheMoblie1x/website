# Coda

## Overview
- Private, previously VC-backed; acquired by Grammarly in an all-stock deal in December 2024. In October 2025, Grammarly itself rebranded as "Superhuman," and Coda now operates as a core product within the Superhuman productivity suite rather than as a standalone independent company.
- Founded in 2014 by CEO Shishir Mehrotra and CTO Alex DeNeui (both former Microsoft/Google/YouTube executives); HQ in San Francisco, CA.
- Employee count estimates vary widely by source, ranging from roughly 130 to 500; a commonly cited figure post-acquisition is in the low hundreds, reflecting integration into the larger Grammarly/Superhuman org.

## Business Model
- Per-seat SaaS subscription model based on "Doc Makers" (users who create/edit docs) rather than all viewers/collaborators.
- Published pricing (2025-2026): Pro plan ~$10/Doc Maker/month, Team plan ~$30/Doc Maker/month, plus a custom-priced Enterprise plan; a free tier also exists for individuals.
- Target customer is primarily mid-market and tech-forward teams (product, ops, and cross-functional teams) that want a flexible "doc + database + app" hybrid, historically differentiated from pure document tools by supporting embedded tables, automations, and building lightweight internal apps.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: docs that behave like relational databases and lightweight applications — Coda blends prose, structured tables ("row" objects), formulas, and buttons/automations into a single canvas, letting teams build internal tools without separate app-building software.
- Available via web and desktop (Mac/Windows) apps, plus iOS/Android mobile apps.
- Positioned above simple note apps (Notion, Google Docs) on structural power (spreadsheet-like data + relational packs/integrations) but below dedicated no-code app builders on ease-of-use; its "docs" angle is really a Trojan horse for structured data and workflow building rather than prose writing.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Estimated 2024 ARR of approximately $41.1 million (up from an estimated $26.8 million in 2023), per third-party revenue-tracking estimates (Latka) — not independently disclosed by Coda/Grammarly, since Coda has never been public.
- Approximately 10,000 customers cited by third-party trackers as of the most recent published estimate.
- Total funding raised prior to acquisition was approximately $240 million across four rounds, with a 2021 Series D valuation of roughly $1.4 billion; the Grammarly acquisition terms (December 2024) were not publicly disclosed. Growth trajectory post-acquisition is tied to Superhuman's broader AI productivity strategy rather than independent Coda metrics.

## Competitive Position
- Coda occupies a differentiated but comparatively small niche as a "doc-database hybrid," competing most directly with Notion (broader consumer/team mindshare) and Airtable (more database-first) rather than head-on with Google Docs or Word.
- Its moat was product depth (formulas, packs/integrations, relational tables) rather than distribution scale, which made it vulnerable as a smaller independent company — the 2024 acquisition by Grammarly reflects consolidation pressure in the crowded docs/productivity space.
- Going forward, Coda's competitive position depends on how well it's integrated into Superhuman's broader AI-first productivity bet rather than standing alone against Notion, Google Docs, or Microsoft Loop.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-Doc-Maker SaaS subscription — Coda's pricing model charges only "Doc Makers" (editors who build docs) rather than every viewer/collaborator, on Free/Pro/Team/Enterprise tiers.
- **Secondary monetization:** Coda AI add-on credits, Packs (third-party integrations/marketplace), and Enterprise-tier admin/security features.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG) — Coda relies on product-led free-tier adoption by individual Doc Makers that expands into paid Team/Enterprise plans, supplemented by direct sales for larger accounts, now under Grammarly/Superhuman's broader go-to-market.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $400–$1,000 (estimated) — self-serve PLG SaaS with a content/community-driven funnel (templates, Coda University) keeping paid acquisition costs moderate relative to sales-led peers.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $2,500–$6,000 (estimated) — assuming a blended ACV of roughly $600–$1,200/year per paying team account and an average customer lifetime of ~4 years.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4:1 (estimated).
- **User demographics:** SMB and mid-market teams, especially ops/product/startup teams wanting a doc-database hybrid; now folded into Superhuman's productivity-suite customer base, skewing tech-forward and North America-heavy.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America, top countries: United States (~55%), Canada (~7%), United Kingdom (~10%), India (~8%), Australia (~5%) — estimated from HQ location (San Francisco) and customer base concentration, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
