# Google Keep

## Overview
- Parent company: Alphabet Inc. / Google LLC (public, NASDAQ: GOOGL). Google Keep is a product within Google Workspace, not an independent subsidiary or business unit.
- Founded / launched: launched March 2013 as a standalone note-taking and list app. Google was founded in 1998 by Larry Page and Sergey Brin; HQ in Mountain View, California.
- Employee count: Alphabet has roughly 181,000 employees company-wide (2024 10-K); no separate headcount is disclosed for the Keep team, which sits within Google Workspace engineering.

## Business Model
- Fully free to end users, monetized indirectly: Keep is bundled at no extra cost with a free Google account and with all paid Google Workspace tiers, functioning as a retention and ecosystem feature rather than a direct revenue line.
- Pricing: Keep itself has no separate price; it rides on Google Workspace plans (Business Starter ~$7/user/month, Business Standard ~$14/user/month, Business Plus ~$22/user/month, per Google's published Workspace pricing) and on Google One storage tiers consumers may need for broader account storage.
- Target customer: mass-market consumers (quick notes, lists, reminders) and, secondarily, Workspace business users who use Keep for lightweight task capture alongside Docs/Sheets/Gmail.

## Product & Positioning
- Differentiators: color-coded sticky-note-style cards, fast capture (voice, image, drawing, checklist), Google Assistant integration, and tight integration with Gmail and Google Docs (notes can be dragged directly into a Doc).
- Platform availability: Android, iOS, Chrome/web (keep.google.com and a Chrome extension); no native desktop (Windows/Mac) app, relying on the web client instead.
- Key features: label-based organization (no folders), collaborative/shared lists, location- and time-based reminders, OCR text extraction from images, and (as of 2026) new AI voice-note features tied to Google's "Live" AI assistant integration announced at Google I/O 2026.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: N/A — Keep generates no direct or disclosed standalone revenue; it supports engagement within Google Workspace, whose revenue Alphabet reports within "Google Cloud" (Workspace) and broader "Google Services," without a Keep-specific breakout. Alphabet's Google Cloud segment (which includes Workspace) reported roughly $43 billion in 2024 revenue.
- Users: no official standalone figure is published; given free bundling with every Google account (over 2 billion Gmail users reported by Google), Keep's potential user exposure is estimated to be in the hundreds of millions, though actual active usage is understood anecdotally to be a small fraction of that given Keep's low profile relative to Docs/Gmail.
- Funding history: N/A — organically built/acquired-feature-set inside Alphabet, a public company; no external funding applies.
- Growth trajectory: growth is tied to Workspace adoption and recent AI feature pushes (voice notes, Gemini-assisted capture) rather than an independently tracked Keep metric.

## Competitive Position
- Keep occupies the "quick capture" niche — simpler and faster than Docs or OneNote, positioned closer to Apple Notes/Simplenote in scope, but it is widely viewed as the most under-invested of the big-tech bundled note apps, receiving fewer major feature updates historically than OneNote or Apple Notes.
- Its moat is Google account ubiquity and Gmail/Docs integration; its weakness is a lack of structure (no nested folders/notebooks) and no native desktop app, which limits it to lightweight use cases rather than serious long-form note-taking.
- Recent AI investment (voice notes, Gemini integration) suggests Google is trying to re-energize Keep as an AI capture surface rather than let it stagnate, but it remains a minor, low-monetization piece of the Workspace portfolio.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Free, bundled with every Google account; no direct revenue. Indirect monetization flows through Google Workspace subscriptions and Google One storage upsells for users who need more account storage.
- **Secondary monetization:** None specific to Keep beyond ecosystem retention — no ads within Keep itself, no in-app purchases; broader Alphabet revenue (Search/ads) is not attributable to Keep usage.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Free/bundled (parent monetizes indirectly) — every Gmail/Google account holder gets Keep automatically; acquisition is entirely a byproduct of Google account sign-ups, not a dedicated funnel.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** ~$0 (estimated). Keep has no standalone acquisition spend; it inherits users for free from the broader Google account base.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** ~$5–$20 (estimated) — a small attributable slice of Google One storage/Workspace upgrade revenue that Keep usage may nudge, given its low profile relative to Gmail/Docs/Drive.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** Not meaningful (estimated) — CAC of ~$0 makes the ratio undefined/infinite; Keep functions purely as an engagement/retention feature, not a monetized unit.
- **User demographics:** Mass-market consumers doing lightweight note/list capture; secondarily Workspace business users. Skews younger/mobile-first given Android-first design and lack of a desktop app. Inferred from positioning rather than disclosed data.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, skewed toward large Android markets given mobile-first design, top countries: United States (~20%), India (~15%), Brazil (~8%), Indonesia (~6%) — estimated from platform reach (Android-first, bundled with Google accounts) and HQ location, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
