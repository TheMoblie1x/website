# LiquidText

## Overview
- Privately held, venture-lite / bootstrapped-leaning startup; not owned by a larger parent company. Total disclosed funding is small (~$250K, per Crunchbase), suggesting the company has been primarily self-sustaining on app revenue since launch.
- Founded 2012 (technology originates from founder Craig Tashman's 2005–2012 Georgia Tech PhD research on "active reading"); HQ in New York, NY. Founder and CEO: Craig Tashman.
- Employee count estimate: ~13 total employees (Crunchbase/Owler estimate).

## Business Model
- Freemium model: a free "Basic" tier plus paid subscription tiers, sold direct-to-consumer through the App Store and directly via liquidtext.net, with a separate enterprise/legal offering.
- Pricing (per Capterra/vendor listings, annualized billing): Live plan ~$3.99/user/month, Live Unlimited ~$5.99/user/month, Pro ~$49.99 (higher tier aimed at professional/legal users). Legal/enterprise pricing is quoted separately for law firms and corporate document-review teams.
- Target customers: students and academic researchers, legal professionals (contract/discovery review), and knowledge workers who need to synthesize long or multiple documents — a more specialized, prosumer/professional audience than general note-taking apps.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: a patented "active reading" canvas that lets users pull excerpts out of PDFs onto a freeform workspace, visually link excerpts to notes, and trace excerpts back to their source location — designed for deep analysis of long/complex documents rather than general PKM.
- Platform availability: iPad (original and still primary platform), iPhone, Mac, and Windows; a legal-specific edition (LiquidText Legal) targets law firm workflows.
- Key features: excerpting/highlighting with visual "strings" connecting notes to source text, document comparison, handwriting/annotation sync, and workspace export — positioned as a document-analysis tool, not a generalized outliner or graph note-taking app.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: not publicly disclosed; estimated to be in the low millions annually based on a ~13-person team, consumer subscription pricing, and over a decade of operation (estimated based on team size and pricing tier volume, not a disclosed figure).
- Downloads: over 1 million cumulative downloads of the iPad app (company/press reporting), with early traction of ~100,000 downloads in its first two weeks post-launch.
- Funding history: approximately $250K raised, with Booga Ventures cited as an investor (Crunchbase). This is a small round relative to company age, consistent with a largely bootstrapped, revenue-funded trajectory.
- Growth trajectory: long-lived (14+ years since original research, 13+ years as a shipped product) with steady but not high-growth-startup-scale headcount, suggesting a stable niche business rather than a hypergrowth venture play.

## Competitive Position
LiquidText occupies a defensible niche adjacent to PDF annotation and PKM tools: it is not trying to be a general-purpose note-taking or graph app (unlike Roam/Logseq/Tana) but a specialized document-analysis workspace for legal, academic, and research use cases. Its moat is a patented interaction model (the excerpt-and-link canvas) and over a decade of Apple ecosystem credibility (multiple "Editor's Choice" placements), but its small team and modest funding limit its ability to expand platform coverage or compete with well-funded PKM/AI note tools entering adjacent territory. It is a durable, profitable-feeling lifestyle/niche business rather than a scale play.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user subscription (Live/Live Unlimited tiers) plus a higher-priced Pro one-time/unlock tier.
- **Secondary monetization:** Separate quote-based Enterprise/Legal edition (LiquidText Legal) sold to law firms and corporate document-review teams — its highest-margin, highest-ACV line; no ads, data licensing, or affiliate revenue disclosed.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — a free Basic tier drives App Store discovery and Apple "Editor's Choice" placements convert browsers into trial users, with a small direct/legal-vertical sales motion layered on top for the Enterprise edition.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $15–$40 (estimated) for the consumer/prosumer subscription tier, consistent with an indie freemium app relying on App Store organic and editorial features rather than paid ads; the Legal/Enterprise edition likely carries a much higher deal-based CAC ($1,000–$3,000+, estimated) given direct outreach to law firms, though the 13-person team suggests this channel is small in volume.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $80–$200 (estimated) for consumer subscribers, assuming ~$5–6/month average blended pricing retained for roughly 2–3 years typical of a prosumer productivity app; Legal/Enterprise seats likely carry much higher LTV ($2,000+, estimated) given the $49.99+ Pro/legal pricing and stickier document-review workflows.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4:1 (estimated) on the consumer side ($140 mid LTV / $28 mid CAC), which looks healthy for an organic-acquisition indie app; the legal-vertical ratio is likely similar or better given higher deal sizes offset by higher acquisition cost.
- **User demographics:** Students and academic researchers (18–30) and legal professionals (attorneys, paralegals, litigation support staff, 25–55) doing contract/discovery review; skews toward Apple-ecosystem (iPad) power users in the US and other English-speaking legal markets. Inferred from product positioning, App Store review industry breakdown (law practice ~14%, higher education/education management ~17% of reviewers per Capterra), and pricing tiers rather than disclosed company data.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America-centric with English-speaking overlay, top countries: United States (~55%), United Kingdom (~12%), Canada (~8%) — estimated from US HQ (Atlanta), Apple App Store legal/academic reviewer base, and English-language-only product, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
