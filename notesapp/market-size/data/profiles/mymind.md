# MyMind

## Overview
- Private, independent, bootstrapped company (mymind, Inc.). Explicitly rejects outside/VC funding to preserve independence.
- Headquartered in New York, NY. Founded by a small independent team (exact founding year not publicly disclosed in available sources; product has been operating since the early 2020s).
- Employee count: approximately 8 people (estimated, per Latka).

## Business Model
- Pure subscription (B2C), no ads, no data resale — explicitly positioned against "free" apps that monetize user data.
- Pricing tiers (from mymind.com): "Student of Life" at $6.99/month, "Mastermind" at $12.99/month; a "Newton" annual plan at $299/year (limited availability). A free guest tier exists with storage-based (not time-based) limits. (Source: mymind.com/mymind-pricing)
- Target customer: individuals seeking a personal, private "second brain" — researchers, designers, writers, and knowledge workers who want AI-organized bookmarking/note-taking without ads or data harvesting.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: automatic AI-based organization (auto-tagging, OCR, visual search) of saved content (articles, images, notes, products, recipes) without manual folders/tags — "it organizes itself."
- Platform availability: web app, macOS/iOS/Android apps, browser extensions (Chrome, Safari, Firefox).
- Key features: AI image tagging, intelligent bookmarking, full-text and visual search, offline-friendly personal archive, strong privacy stance (no ad-based monetization).

## Financial Profile (estimated)
- Revenue: estimated ~$880K ARR (Latka, 2025 estimate) — small, niche subscription business.
- Adoption: user counts not publicly disclosed; positioned as a niche/cult product rather than mass-market.
- Funding: none — self-funded/bootstrapped by design; company has publicly stated it turns down outside investment to avoid investor obligations.
- Growth trajectory: steady, small-team growth; no signs of aggressive scaling or fundraising plans.

## Competitive Position
mymind occupies a small, defensible niche as a privacy-first, aesthetically distinct personal knowledge/bookmarking tool, competing loosely with Notion, Evernote, Pocket, and Raindrop.io but differentiating on automatic AI organization and an anti-ad-model stance. Its moat is more cultural/brand (privacy, design taste, community loyalty) than technological or scale-based, and its small team and bootstrapped model cap its ability to out-invest larger, VC-backed competitors in AI R&D.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Pure subscription (B2C monthly/annual plans).
- **Secondary monetization:** None disclosed — explicitly anti-ads, no data resale; no add-on or enterprise tier.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — relies on word-of-mouth, design/privacy-focused positioning, and a free guest tier to convert niche/cult-following users into paid subscribers rather than paid ads or outbound sales.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $25–$90 (estimated). Small bootstrapped team (~8 people) with no disclosed paid-acquisition spend; low CAC consistent with organic/content-driven growth for an indie consumer subscription product.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $150–$400 (estimated). Based on ~$7–$13/month plans with an assumed 2–3 year average retention for a "personal archive" product with high switching costs once content is stored.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4:1 (estimated) — healthy for a bootstrapped subscription business, though unverified.
- **User demographics:** Individual knowledge workers — designers, writers, researchers — privacy-conscious and design-sensitive, skewing 25–45, with likely concentration in US/Europe urban creative/tech hubs.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America-led with a substantial Europe base, top countries: United States (~45%), United Kingdom (~12%), Germany (~8%) — estimated from HQ location (New York) and design/tech-hub-skewing demographic profile, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
