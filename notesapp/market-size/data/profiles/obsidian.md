# Obsidian

## Overview
- Privately held, bootstrapped company (Dynalist Inc.); no outside venture funding.
- Developed by Dynalist Inc., founded by Shida Li (CTO) and Erica Xu (COO), who met at the University of Waterloo and previously built Dynalist (an outliner app). Obsidian launched in 2020. Company is based in Oakville, Ontario, Canada.
- Employee count estimate: ~18 employees as of 2025 (widely reported, not officially confirmed by the company).

## Business Model
- Freemium/optional-payment model: the app itself is entirely free for personal use, with no paywalled features and no usage limits.
- Revenue comes from optional paid add-on services and an optional commercial license: Obsidian Sync (~$4–$8/mo depending on tier/storage) and Obsidian Publish (~$8–$16/mo) are the primary paid services; a commercial-use license (~$50/year per user) is requested but not technically enforced for companies using Obsidian at work.
- Target customer: individual "prosumer" knowledge workers, students, researchers, and PKM (personal knowledge management) enthusiasts; not enterprise-sales-driven.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: local-first, plain-text Markdown storage (files live on the user's device, not locked in a proprietary cloud format), combined with a bidirectional-linking "graph view" for networked note-taking (a Zettelkasten/PKM approach).
- Platform availability: Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android — all with local file access and optional end-to-end encrypted sync.
- Key features: extensive community plugin ecosystem (thousands of community plugins and themes), canvas (visual/whiteboard mode), graph view, no lock-in (open file formats), strong offline-first architecture.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: not publicly disclosed. Third-party estimates cluster around ~$2M ARR (2025–2026, per Getlatka/OperatorBook aggregation), though these are unverified; a viral 2026 social post speculated as high as $25M ARR, which OperatorBook and others treat as unreliable.
- Funding: $0 — company is self-funded/bootstrapped from Sync/Publish/license revenue and reportedly profitable.
- Users/downloads: exact figures not disclosed by the company; commonly cited as having a large, highly engaged PKM/developer-adjacent user base (millions of downloads across platforms, per third-party trackers, unverified).
- Growth trajectory: no disclosed growth metrics; company messaging emphasizes sustainability and independence over rapid scaling.

## Competitive Position
Obsidian owns the "local-first, plain-text PKM power-user" niche, directly competing with Roam Research, Logseq, and (at the edges) Notion for the note-taking-as-thinking-tool audience. Its moat is a passionate community plugin ecosystem and a credible non-lock-in promise (your notes are just files), which is difficult for VC-backed, cloud-native competitors to replicate, though its narrow monetization (small paid add-ons only) caps its revenue ceiling relative to SaaS peers.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Optional paid add-on subscriptions (Obsidian Sync, Obsidian Publish) layered on a free core app.
- **Secondary monetization:** Commercial-use license (~$50/year/user, requested but not enforced); no ads, no data monetization.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — almost entirely community-driven: Reddit (top 5% community), Discord (110K+ members), YouTube/PKM-creator word of mouth, and the plugin ecosystem itself acting as a growth loop; no paid advertising.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $5–$20 (estimated). Classic indie-freemium-via-organic archetype — the free core app has zero marketing spend, and community/content efforts are the only quasi-acquisition cost, so CAC sits near the bottom of the indie freemium band.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $150–$350 (estimated) — e.g., Sync at ~$8/mo × ~24–30-month avg retention ≈ $200–$240 for the minority of users who convert to a paid add-on (most users never pay, so this is LTV of a paying customer, not blended across the whole base).
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~15:1 (estimated) — very healthy for the paying subset, though overall revenue ceiling is capped by the small fraction of the (mostly free) user base that ever converts.
- **User demographics:** Inferred from positioning/community: developers and other technical/power users, writers, and PKM/Zettelkasten enthusiasts, roughly 25–45, skewing male and technically sophisticated, with a global but developer-hub-concentrated (US/Europe) geography; no official demographic data disclosed.

- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, top countries: United States (~30%), Germany (~10%), United Kingdom (~8%), Canada (~6%), India (~6%) — estimated from platform reach and developer-community concentration (Reddit/Discord skew toward US/Western Europe dev hubs), plus HQ location, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
