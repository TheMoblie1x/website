# GitBook

## Overview
- Privately held, VC-backed (seed stage) company; not owned by a larger parent.
- Founded 2014 by Samy Pesse and Aaron O'Mullan. HQ in Paris/San Francisco (distributed team).
- Employee count estimate: roughly 35-45 employees (2026 estimates from LeadIQ/Latka range 35-44), a lean team relative to its ~450,000-user base.

## Business Model
- SaaS subscription, billed per documentation "space"/site, with an additional per-seat fee for collaborators.
- Pricing tiers (as of 2026, cited from GitBook and aggregator sources): Free for individuals, open-source, and non-profits; Premium at $65/month; Ultimate at $249/month; each additional collaborator seat adds roughly $12/month on paid plans.
- Target customer: developer-focused teams and technical product/docs teams at startups and mid-size software companies; strong pull with open-source projects due to free tier.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: Git-based, version-controlled documentation authoring combined with a polished, non-technical-friendly WYSIWYG editor — positions itself as a bridge between "docs as code" and easy collaborative editing.
- Platform availability: web app, with two-way Git sync (GitHub/GitLab) for engineering teams who want docs to live alongside code.
- Key features: real-time collaborative editing, API reference generation (OpenAPI import), AI-assisted writing/search features, custom domains, and public/private space permissions.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: estimated ~$3.9M ARR as of 2025 (source: Latka/getlatka.com estimate; not officially disclosed by GitBook).
- Customers: GitBook claims roughly 450,000 registered users across free and paid tiers (company-reported); paying-customer count not disclosed.
- Funding history: Raised a $940K seed round (investors reported to include Fly Ventures and Point Nine Capital); some trackers show total disclosed funding in the low single-digit millions ($150K-$1M+ depending on source, likely reflecting incomplete public records). No large Series A/B has been publicly announced as of 2026 — GitBook appears to have grown largely through revenue rather than heavy VC funding.
- Growth trajectory: Steady, profitable-leaning growth on a small team; no signs of hypergrowth funding rounds, suggesting a capital-efficient, product-led growth strategy.

## Competitive Position
GitBook occupies a mid-market niche between free/open-source wikis (Outline, Docusaurus) and heavyweight enterprise knowledge platforms (Confluence, Glean) — its moat is the polished Git-native editing experience that appeals to developer-centric teams, but it lacks the scale, enterprise sales motion, or AI depth of newer entrants like Mintlify or ReadMe in the API-docs niche. Its lean funding history suggests resilience but limited resources to out-invest better-capitalized competitors in AI features.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-seat/per-editor SaaS subscription (Free, Plus, Pro, Enterprise tiers)
- **Secondary monetization:** Enterprise-tier add-ons (SSO, advanced permissions, custom domains, higher AI usage limits); no ads
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG) — free tier and generous docs-as-a-product experience drive bottom-up signup, with inside sales layered on for team/enterprise upgrades.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $500–$1,800 (estimated) — dev-tool PLG motion with low-touch self-serve conversion; upper end reflects inside-sales-assisted enterprise deals.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $3,000–$9,000 (estimated) — assumes blended ACV of ~$1,000–$3,000/year across free-to-paid conversions and a ~3-year average customer lifetime.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~5:1 (estimated)
- **User demographics:** Small-to-mid-sized software teams, technical writers, and DevRel/engineering teams; skews startup and scale-up companies, global but US/Europe-weighted.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global, US/Europe-weighted, top countries: United States (~35%), France (~12%), Germany (~8%) — estimated from HQ location (Paris/San Francisco) and dev-tool community reach, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
