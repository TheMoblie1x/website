# Simplenote

## Overview
- Parent company: Automattic Inc. (private, VC-backed; the company behind WordPress.com, Tumblr, WooCommerce, Pocket Casts, Day One, and other properties). Simplenote was originally built by Simperium, a startup Automattic acquired in 2013.
- Founded / launched: originally launched in 2008 by Simperium; acquired by Automattic in 2013 and has operated as an Automattic product since. Automattic was founded in 2005 by Matt Mullenweg; HQ is distributed/remote-first, with a nominal base in San Francisco, California.
- Employee count: Automattic employs roughly 1,700 people company-wide (self-reported, distributed workforce across ~90 countries); Simplenote itself is maintained by a very small team, and as of March 2026 Automattic announced active development has ended, with only basic bug-fix maintenance planned going forward.

## Business Model
- Completely free with no premium tier, no ads, and no in-app purchases — an unusual model among the profiled companies. It generates no direct revenue.
- Pricing: $0 across all platforms; there is no paid plan.
- Target customer: consumers and prosumers who want a minimalist, distraction-free plain-text note app, particularly technical/power users who value simplicity, speed, and text-file portability over rich formatting.

## Product & Positioning
- Differentiators: radical simplicity — plain-text notes only (no images, no rich formatting, no folders in the traditional sense), fast full-text search, note versioning/history, and tag-based organization instead of folders.
- Platform availability: iOS, Android, macOS, Windows, Linux, and web — broad cross-platform support, including open-source desktop/web clients.
- Key features: automatic sync via Simperium's backend, markdown preview support, note-history/version restore, collaborative note sharing via publish-to-web links, and an open-source codebase for several of its clients.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: N/A / $0 — Simplenote generates no direct revenue and is treated by Automattic as a free community/goodwill product rather than a monetized line of business; it is not material to Automattic's overall financials.
- Users: no official current figure is published; historical estimates place Simplenote's user base in the low millions of registered users, with download counts on Google Play/App Store in the millions-of-installs range (app-store listing data), though Automattic has not disclosed active-user figures in recent years.
- Funding history: N/A directly — Simplenote itself was acquired (terms undisclosed) as part of Automattic's 2013 purchase of Simperium. Automattic, the parent, is a private VC-backed company that has raised over $1 billion across multiple rounds (investors including Insight Partners, Tiger Global, Salesforce Ventures, BlackRock, and others), with past reported valuations exceeding $7 billion (2021 round); no funding is attributable to Simplenote specifically since it is a fully-owned free product, not a separate financed entity.
- Growth trajectory: flat-to-declining investment — as of March 2026, Automattic confirmed active feature development has ended, with the product kept alive only for maintenance/bug fixes, signaling it is no longer a growth priority within Automattic's portfolio.

## Competitive Position
- Simplenote occupies a narrow but loyal niche: the "notepad for people who hate note-taking apps" — minimal friction, plain text, fast sync, and free forever, which wins favor among developers, writers, and anti-clutter users.
- It has essentially no monetization and, per Automattic's 2026 announcement, no active development roadmap, meaning its competitive position is best described as a legacy utility maintained out of goodwill rather than a growing product competing for new users.
- Its moat is its simplicity and reliability rather than features; it is not a serious competitor to feature-rich tools like Evernote, OneNote, or Notion, but remains relevant to a specific minimalist-user segment.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** None — fully free, no subscription, no ads, no in-app purchases; effectively donation-funded in spirit (subsidized as a goodwill/community product within Automattic's broader portfolio, though not literally donation-based).
- **Secondary monetization:** None disclosed or evident; Automattic confirmed in March 2026 that active development has ended, reinforcing that Simplenote is not treated as a monetization vehicle.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion (organic only, no conversion funnel) — users find it via App Store/Google Play search and word-of-mouth among minimalist/technical users; no paid acquisition or sales motion.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $2–$10 (estimated) — at the very low end of the indie freemium consumer-app archetype, reflecting pure App Store/organic discovery with zero marketing spend, now further reduced since Automattic has stopped active investment.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** ~$0 (estimated) — with no monetization whatsoever, there is no revenue to attribute to any user relationship regardless of retention length.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~0:1 (estimated) — unhealthy by conventional business metrics, but irrelevant here since Simplenote was never built to be a revenue-generating unit; it exists as a low-cost goodwill/utility product.
- **User demographics:** Technical/power users, writers, developers, and students who prioritize plain-text simplicity and portability over rich features; skews toward existing Automattic/WordPress-adjacent and open-source-affine communities. Inferred from product positioning and review commentary rather than disclosed data.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, skewed toward developer-heavy markets, top countries: United States (~30%), United Kingdom (~9%), Germany (~8%), India (~7%) — estimated from Automattic's distributed/remote-first HQ model and typical developer-tool geography, not disclosed.
