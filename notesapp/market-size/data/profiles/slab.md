# Slab

## Overview
- Privately held; independent, not acquired. Notably profitable and largely self-sustaining since its only funding round.
- Founded in 2017 by Jason Chen, Francisco Azócar, Chengyin Liu, and additional co-founders; headquartered in San Francisco, CA.
- Employee count estimate: small team, roughly 20-40 employees (estimated based on lean, revenue-funded growth profile typical of companies at Slab's reported ARR).

## Business Model
- Per-user SaaS subscription with a free tier for small teams and two paid tiers.
- Pricing (as of 2025-2026, billed annually): Free for up to 10 users; Startup plan at $6.67/user/month (unlimited users, includes AI Autofix); Business plan at $12.50/user/month (adds full AI suite, SAML SSO, SCIM provisioning, GraphQL API). (Source: slab.com/pricing, aggregated via vendr.com.)
- Target customer: SMB and mid-market technology teams; notable customers cited include Asana, MongoDB, Postman, and Shipt.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: clean, opinionated internal wiki UX with strong emphasis on unified search across integrated third-party tools (Slack, GitHub, Google Drive, Salesforce, etc.), positioned as a Confluence alternative for modern, product-led teams.
- Platform availability: web app with browser extension for unified search; integrations-heavy architecture rather than native mobile-first design.
- Key features: unified/federated search across connected apps, topic organization, version history, and AI-assisted content tools (AI Autofix, AI summaries) layered into paid tiers.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed)
- Disclosed: Slab reached approximately $3M ARR in 2024, up from $2.2M ARR in 2023, across 1,000+ paying customers (source: aggregated industry reporting).
- Funding history: raised a single Series A in 2018 (amount not consistently disclosed across sources; estimated in the $5-10M range) and has not raised additional venture funding since — an unusual profile of nearly eight years of revenue-funded operation.
- No disclosed valuation; company is characterized as profitable and low-risk of forced sale, pivot, or shutdown given its capital-efficient model.
- Growth trajectory: steady, moderate ARR growth (~35% YoY 2023→2024 per disclosed figures) rather than hypergrowth, consistent with a bootstrapped-after-Series-A posture.

## Competitive Position
Slab is a durable, profitable niche player rather than a category leader — its lack of continued VC funding limits its ability to out-market or out-build larger rivals like Confluence, Notion, or Guru, but also insulates it from down-round or shutdown risk. Its moat is customer loyalty built on search quality and simplicity rather than platform lock-in or network effects, making it a stable but not dominant competitor in the space.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-seat SaaS subscription (Startup/Business tiers) with a free tier for small teams.
- **Secondary monetization:** AI feature add-ons (AI Autofix, AI summaries) and admin/security features (SAML SSO, SCIM, GraphQL API) gated to the Business tier as an upsell path.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG) — free-tier funnel drives initial adoption, with a light sales touch as teams scale to Business-tier needs.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $500–$2,000 (estimated) — SMB/mid-market PLG motion run by a small (20–40 person), capital-efficient team with no evidence of heavy paid acquisition.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $3,000–$9,000 (estimated) — assuming a typical paying team ACV of ~$1,500–$3,000 (mid-size team at $6.67–$12.50/seat/month) and a 3–5 year average lifetime, supported by Slab's demonstrated steady, low-churn ARR growth (~35% YoY 2023→2024) on a profitable, non-VC-refueled base.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4:1 (estimated) — healthy and consistent with a company that has grown organically off a single Series A since 2018.
- **User demographics:** SMB and mid-market technology companies; primary buyers are engineering/product teams; notable customers include Asana, MongoDB, Postman, and Shipt; US-centric (San Francisco HQ).
- **Estimated user geography:** North America-dominant, top countries: United States (~65%), Canada (~10%), United Kingdom (~8%), Germany (~6%) — estimated from San Francisco HQ and named US tech-company customer base, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
