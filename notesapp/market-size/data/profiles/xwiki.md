# XWiki (open-source/commercial)

## Overview
- Dual model: XWiki is an open-source project (LGPL) governed by the XWiki.org community, commercially backed by XWiki SAS, a private French company.
- Founded 2004 in France (first commit December 2003, public release January 2004) by Ludovic Dubost (CEO), with Oren Gampel and Idan Rooze; Vincent Massol became lead developer in 2006.
- Employee count: approximately 68 people (2026 estimate, per Latka).

## Business Model
- Open-core: the underlying XWiki platform is free/open-source (self-hostable "Community" edition); XWiki SAS monetizes via paid cloud hosting (XWiki Cloud) and on-premises support/enterprise tiers.
- Pricing tiers (xwiki.com/en/pricing, per-user/month, exact totals require entering team size on the site):
  - Community: free, self-hosted, self-managed.
  - Starter: up to 25 users, 2GB storage, cloud-only, support portal access.
  - Basic: up to 1,000 users, 2–20GB storage, cloud or on-prem, ~1-business-day response.
  - Business: up to 10,000 users, 5–100GB storage, cloud or on-prem, includes a free Confluence Migration Toolkit.
  - Enterprise: unlimited users, 10–500GB storage, dedicated account manager, 2-business-hour critical response.
  - (Third-party pricing trackers cite illustrative per-user rates around €1.74–€2.94/user/month across tiers.)
- Target customer: mid-size to large organizations (often in Europe/public sector) wanting an open-source, self-hostable alternative to Confluence with data-sovereignty guarantees.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: structured-wiki platform combining wiki pages with a built-in scripting/application layer (XWiki apps), letting users build lightweight business applications on top of wiki content — not just documentation.
- Platform availability: self-hosted (on-prem, any Java-compatible server) or XWiki Cloud (managed SaaS); explicit "digital sovereignty" positioning for EU customers wary of US-hosted alternatives.
- Key features: structured data/app-building on wiki pages, granular rights management, extensive extension marketplace, multilingual support, Confluence migration tooling, LGPL licensing allowing commercial derivative use.

## Financial Profile
- Revenue: estimated $4M–$7.1M annually (sources diverge: Latka reports ~$4M bootstrapped revenue; other estimators cite $6.7–7.1M) — labeled as estimated given the spread.
- Adoption: exact install/customer counts not publicly disclosed; long-standing community project since 2004 with an active open-source ecosystem and extension marketplace.
- Funding: no venture capital — bootstrapped/self-funded since 2004; received one disclosed grant of $148,000 in September 2021. No rounds listed on Crunchbase/PitchBook.
- Growth trajectory: slow, steady, profitable-oriented growth typical of a bootstrapped European B2B software company; recent positioning around Confluence-migration and EU data sovereignty suggests a growth push tied to European enterprises reducing reliance on US SaaS.

## Competitive Position
XWiki competes directly with Confluence as an open-source, self-hostable, EU-sovereign alternative, differentiating through its "wiki as application platform" architecture and dedicated Confluence-migration tooling. Its moat is modest — a 20+ year head start, an LGPL license permitting broad adoption, and strong EU/data-sovereignty positioning — but it lacks Atlassian's ecosystem scale, and its bootstrapped nature limits R&D and go-to-market investment relative to well-funded competitors.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Open-core cloud+support — free/open-source Community edition, monetized via paid XWiki Cloud hosting and on-premises support/enterprise tiers.
- **Secondary monetization:** Enterprise support contracts (Basic/Business/Enterprise tiers with escalating SLA response times); Confluence Migration Toolkit bundled as a value-add to win migrations.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG) — free Community edition and self-serve Starter tier drive top-of-funnel adoption, with an inside sales/account-manager motion for Business/Enterprise deals (especially European public-sector and data-sovereignty-sensitive buyers).
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $800–$2,500 (estimated) for paying support/cloud customers — open-core, self-hosted+support model requiring sales engineering and migration support (e.g., Confluence Migration Toolkit) to close mid-to-large accounts; ~$0 for free self-hosted Community-edition users.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $3,000–$15,000 (estimated), assuming ~€2–3/user/month across a multi-year enterprise contract (3–5 years) with dozens to hundreds of seats; scales substantially higher for Business/Enterprise tiers with 1,000–10,000+ users.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~4:1 to 6:1 (estimated) for paying customers.
- **User demographics:** Mid-size to large organizations, often in Europe and public sector, seeking an open-source, data-sovereign Confluence alternative; buyer persona is IT/knowledge-management leadership.
- **Estimated user geography:** Europe-led, EU-sovereignty-driven, top countries: France (~30%), Germany (~15%), United Kingdom (~8%) — estimated from HQ location (France) and explicit EU digital-sovereignty positioning targeting European public-sector buyers, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
