# Glean

## Overview
- Private company, VC-backed (no parent company); one of the most well-funded enterprise AI search startups.
- Founded in 2019 by Arvind Jain (former Google distinguished engineer) along with several ex-Google engineers; HQ in Palo Alto, California.
- Employee count estimate: approximately 1,648 employees as of May 2026 (source: Tracxn/company data aggregation) — reflects rapid headcount growth alongside revenue growth.

## Business Model
- Enterprise SaaS, sold via annual contracts (usage/seat-based enterprise licensing, not self-serve).
- Pricing: Not publicly listed; initial contract values (ICVs) typically range from $100K-$500K annually for mid-market/enterprise customers, with Fortune 500 enterprise deals reportedly exceeding $5M annually (source: industry/analyst estimates).
- Target customer: Mid-market and enterprise organizations (500-2,000+ employees), with a focus on technology companies initially, expanding to broader enterprise verticals.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: AI-powered enterprise search and "work assistant" that unifies search, knowledge discovery, and increasingly AI agent capabilities across a company's entire SaaS/data stack (Slack, Confluence, Google Drive, Salesforce, GitHub, etc.).
- Platform availability: Web app, browser extension, Slack/Teams integrations, and API/agent platform (Glean Agents).
- Key features: Permission-aware unified search across 100+ connectors, generative AI answers grounded in enterprise data, and an expanding enterprise AI agent-building platform — positioning itself beyond search into the broader "enterprise AI platform" category.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: Reported to have hit $200M ARR in December 2025 (up from $100M nine months earlier), and reached approximately $300M ARR by May 2026 (+89% YoY) — sourced from Fortune and Latka estimates; strong, well-documented hypergrowth.
- Customers: Approximately 200 enterprise customers reported (as of recent funding announcements); early/anchor customers included Databricks, Canva, and Confluent.
- Funding history (well-publicized, real figures): Raised a $150M Series F in June 2025 led by Wellington Management at a $7.2B valuation, with participation from Khosla Ventures, Bicycle Capital, Geodesic Capital, Archerman Capital, Altimeter, Capital One Ventures, Citi, Coatue, DST Global, General Catalyst, ICONIQ, IVP, Kleiner Perkins, Latitude Capital, Lightspeed Venture Partners, Sapphire Ventures, and Sequoia Capital. This came just nine months after a $260M Series E that had already doubled its valuation to $4.6B (from a prior round). Sources: BusinessWire, CNBC, Glean press release.
- Growth trajectory: Among the fastest-growing enterprise AI companies by ARR and valuation trajectory (valuation roughly 56x its $150M Series F check size within a year of the prior round), reflecting strong investor conviction in the enterprise AI-agent thesis.

## Competitive Position
Glean is a clear category leader in AI-native enterprise search/work-assistant software, with a valuation ($7.2B) and ARR trajectory ($300M+) that dwarf all other companies in this batch — its moat comes from deep, permission-aware integrations across enterprise SaaS tools and a head start in enterprise AI agent tooling. It faces long-term competitive pressure from Microsoft Copilot, Google's enterprise AI offerings, and other well-funded AI search entrants, but currently sits at the top of the enterprise knowledge/search market by both capital raised and revenue scale.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Enterprise SaaS, per-seat licensing with minimum user commitments (often 100–250 seats) plus Enterprise Flex pooled usage credits
- **Secondary monetization:** Pay-per-use AI/agent credits under Enterprise Flex for advanced feature consumption beyond the per-seat allowance; professional services/implementation for large deployments; no ads
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Sales-led enterprise — annual (often multi-year) contracts starting around $60K and scaling into the millions, closed through direct enterprise sales with named-account outreach and RFP processes.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $15,000–$60,000 (estimated) — enterprise sales-led motion with long sales cycles, POCs, and security/procurement review typical of six- and seven-figure contracts.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $300,000–$1,000,000 (estimated) — assumes ~$100K–$200K average annual contract value and a ~4–5 year enterprise retention/expansion lifetime, aided by high switching costs.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~15:1 (estimated)
- **User demographics:** Large enterprises and mid-market companies; IT, knowledge management, and increasingly AI/platform teams are the primary buyers; US/global enterprise skew, tech-forward organizations over-indexed.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America, top countries: United States (~70%), United Kingdom (~8%), Canada (~5%), India (~4%) — estimated from HQ location (Palo Alto, CA) and enterprise customer base, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
