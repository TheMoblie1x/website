# Bear

## Overview
- Parent company: Shiny Frog Ltd., a small independent, bootstrapped software studio (private, not VC-backed). Founded and led by CEO Danilo Bonardi.
- Founded / launched: Bear was released in 2016; Bear 2 (a substantial rewrite/relaunch) shipped for macOS, iOS, and iPadOS in July 2023. Shiny Frog is a small studio without a widely publicized separate founding date distinct from the app's launch.
- Employee count: estimated at approximately 3 employees as of 2026 (per third-party company-data aggregator Latka), consistent with Bear's positioning as a small, independent, tightly-scoped team.

## Business Model
- Freemium subscription: a functional free tier plus a paid "Bear Pro" subscription that unlocks cross-device sync, additional themes/export options, and organizational features.
- Pricing: Bear Pro is $2.99/month or $29.99/year (per app-store and third-party listings as of 2026); no enterprise or team tier is offered, reflecting its focus on individual users rather than business customers.
- Target customer: individual consumers and prosumers — particularly writers, developers, and Apple-ecosystem power users who value a polished, markdown-native writing experience over collaboration or enterprise features.

## Product & Positioning
- Differentiators: a markdown-first, typography-focused writing experience with a strong design aesthetic, hashtag-based nested tagging (used in place of traditional folders), and native, well-optimized performance on Apple platforms.
- Platform availability: iOS, iPadOS, macOS only — no Android, Windows, or web client, making it exclusively an Apple-ecosystem product (similar in scope-limitation to Apple Notes, but as a third-party paid app rather than pre-installed).
- Key features: cross-note linking, code-block syntax highlighting, export to multiple formats (PDF, HTML, DOCX, JSON), Apple Pencil/handwriting support on iPad, and a widely praised, minimalist UI design.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")

- Users: no official user or download count is publicly disclosed by Shiny Frog; given its long tenure (since 2016) and consistent App Store visibility in the productivity category, active users are reasonably estimated in the low hundreds of thousands to low millions, but this is not a disclosed figure.
- Funding history: N/A / bootstrapped — Bear/Shiny Frog is not known to have raised institutional VC funding; it operates as a small, self-funded independent studio. Latka estimates a valuation of roughly $990K based on revenue multiples, which is an unofficial third-party estimate, not a disclosed valuation.
- Growth trajectory: steady, modest growth consistent with a small independent team; the Bear 2 relaunch (2023) was the most significant recent product investment, aimed at refreshing the tech stack and sync infrastructure rather than pursuing aggressive user-acquisition growth.

## Competitive Position
- Bear competes on design and writing-experience quality rather than scale — it is a favorite among a devoted niche of writers and developers who prioritize a beautiful, fast, markdown-native app over cross-platform reach or collaboration features.
- Its lack of Android/Windows/web support is a structural ceiling on its addressable market, and its tiny team size limits the pace at which it can add enterprise-grade features (no real collaboration, no admin/team tier).
- Its moat is brand loyalty and design polish within the Apple ecosystem rather than any technical or distribution advantage; it remains a lifestyle/indie product rather than a scaled business, and is more exposed to competitive and platform risk than any other company in this set.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user subscription (Bear Pro, $2.99/month or $29.99/year) sold direct-to-consumer via the Apple App Store.
- **Secondary monetization:** None — no enterprise/team tier, no ads, no data licensing; monetization is entirely the single Pro subscription tier.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — a functional free tier drives App Store discovery and organic/word-of-mouth growth among writers and developers, converting to Pro for sync and export features; no evidence of paid-ads spend given the tiny team size.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $10–$40 (estimated) — squarely in the indie freemium consumer-app band, reflecting App Store organic discovery and design-community word-of-mouth rather than paid acquisition, appropriate for a ~3-person bootstrapped studio.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $60–$100 (estimated) — e.g., ~$2.50/mo effective (annual plan) × ~30-month average retention ≈ $75, consistent with a loyal niche subscriber base.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~3:1 (estimated) — at the healthy threshold, plausible for a small, low-overhead indie studio that doesn't need aggressive paid acquisition to sustain its ~$330K ARR.
- **User demographics:** Individual consumers and prosumers — writers, developers, and Apple-ecosystem power users, skewing 25–45, design-conscious and willing to pay for a polished experience; concentrated in North America/Western Europe given Apple-exclusive, English-first positioning. Inferred from positioning; no disclosed demographic data.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America/Europe, typical of an indie Apple-ecosystem developer tool, top countries: United States (~35%), United Kingdom (~10%), Germany (~8%), Italy (~6%) — estimated from Shiny Frog's small independent-studio profile and Apple-exclusive, English-first positioning, not disclosed.
