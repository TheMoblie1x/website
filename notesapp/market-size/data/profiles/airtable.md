# Airtable

## Overview
- Private, venture-backed company; no parent (VC-backed, independent).
- Founded in 2012 by Howie Liu, Andrew Ofstad, and Emmett Nicholas; headquartered in San Francisco, California.
- Employee count: approximately 700 as of 2026, down from roughly 947 in 2025 (estimated, aggregator-sourced) — reflects layoffs/rightsizing common among late-stage SaaS companies in 2024-2025. Source: [sqmagazine.co.uk](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/how-many-people-work-at-airtable/).

## Business Model
- SaaS subscription with per-seat ("editor") pricing; free tier plus tiered paid plans; viewers/commenters/form-submitters are free across all tiers.
- Pricing (2026, per editor/month, billed annually): Free ($0, 5 editors, 1,000 records/base), Team ($20/mo, or $24 monthly, 50,000 records/base), Business ($45/mo, or $54 monthly, 125,000 records/base), Enterprise Scale (custom pricing). Add-ons include Airtable Portals (~$120-150/mo for 15 guests) and AI credit packs (~$40/mo for 20K credits). Source: [airtable.com/pricing](https://airtable.com/pricing), [jetadmin.io](https://www.jetadmin.io/blog/airtable-pricing-in-2026-plans-real-costs-and-when-to-upgrade/).
- Target customer: broad — SMBs up through large enterprise; reports ~80% of the Fortune 100 use Airtable, positioning it as a horizontal, cross-functional operations/database tool rather than a niche vertical product.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: spreadsheet-database hybrid ("no-code database") that lets non-technical users build custom apps, CRMs, project trackers, and internal tools without code, combined with automations, interfaces, and increasingly AI-driven workflow features.
- Platform availability: web, native macOS/Windows desktop apps, iOS/Android mobile apps, extensive API and integration ecosystem (Zapier, Slack, Salesforce, Google Workspace, etc.).
- Key features: relational linked records, multiple views (grid, kanban, calendar, gallery, Gantt), Interface Designer for building lightweight apps, Automations, and Airtable AI for summarization/generation within bases.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: ARR reported at roughly $440M (end of 2025) to $478M-$480M (mid-2026), up modestly (~16% YoY) — growth has slowed markedly from Airtable's earlier hypergrowth years. ~90% gross margins reported as of August 2025. Source: [getlatka.com](https://getlatka.com/companies/airtable), [sacra.com](https://sacra.com/c/airtable/).
- Customers: reportedly over 500,000 organizations use the platform.
- Funding history: raised approximately $1.4B in total funding; last primary-round valuation was $11.7B (2021 Series F), but secondary-market/reported valuation had contracted to roughly $4B by late 2025 — a decline of about 66%, reflecting a broader markdown of 2021-era SaaS valuations. Source: [sqmagazine.co.uk — Airtable Statistics](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/airtable-statistics/), [PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/100744-84).
- Growth trajectory: revenue growth has decelerated into the mid-teens percentage range, and headcount has shrunk — consistent with a company defending its valuation markdown while pushing AI features to reaccelerate growth.

## Competitive Position
Airtable is the category-defining no-code database/spreadsheet-hybrid platform, with strong enterprise penetration and brand recognition, but it now faces intensifying competition from Notion (horizontal workspace consolidation), Monday.com and ClickUp (work-management platforms adding database features), and native AI-app-builder tools. Its moat is switching costs from deeply embedded custom workflows/bases inside large organizations, but the sharp valuation markdown signals investors see slowing growth and margin/competitive pressure rather than a durable, expanding moat.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-seat SaaS subscription (editor-based pricing across Free/Team/Business/Enterprise Scale tiers).
- **Secondary monetization:** Add-ons including Airtable Portals (external guest access, ~$120-150/mo) and AI credit packs (~$40/mo for 20K credits), plus premium Enterprise Scale contracts with custom pricing and security features.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG), blending into sales-led enterprise motion — SMB/team plans convert bottom-up from the free tier, while large accounts (Fortune 100 penetration) are landed and expanded via direct sales and account management.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $400–$1,200 (estimated). Airtable's horizontal, cross-functional positioning and broader enterprise sales motion push CAC above pure-PLG dev tools like Linear, but its large existing installed base and inbound brand recognition keep it below fully sales-led competitors.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $8,000–$20,000 (estimated), assuming an average paying account of ~15–30 editors at $20-45/editor/month (~$3,600-$16,000 ACV) retained roughly 4-5 years, reflecting high switching costs once bases are embedded in workflows, tempered by the recent growth deceleration and valuation markdown.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~10:1 (estimated) — healthy on paper, though slowing revenue growth (~16% YoY) suggests newer cohorts may carry weaker economics than legacy accounts.
- **User demographics:** Broad SMB-to-enterprise base; reportedly ~80% of the Fortune 100 use Airtable, spanning operations, marketing, product, and IT teams building custom internal tools — global, English-language-skewed enterprise usage.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America-dominant with meaningful global enterprise reach, top countries: United States (~50%), United Kingdom (~9%), Canada (~6%), Australia (~5%) — estimated from HQ location / platform reach, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
