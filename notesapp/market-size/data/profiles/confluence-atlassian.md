# Confluence (Atlassian)

## Overview
- Public subsidiary product of Atlassian Corporation (NASDAQ: TEAM). Atlassian itself is publicly traded, incorporated in Delaware with a registered office in the UK and operational HQ presence in Sydney, Australia and San Francisco, CA.
- Atlassian founded in 2002 by Mike Cannon-Brookes and Scott Farquhar in Sydney; Confluence launched in 2004 as a wiki-style collaboration product extending Atlassian's reach from developer tools (Jira) to broader business/knowledge-work teams.
- Atlassian companywide employee count: ~13,800 employees (FY2025). Confluence-specific headcount is not separately disclosed.

## Business Model
- Per-user SaaS subscription (Cloud) plus legacy Data Center/on-prem licensing (Atlassian is sunsetting Server and phasing down Data Center in favor of Cloud). Sold standalone or bundled with Jira and other Atlassian products.
- Pricing (Cloud, 2026): Free tier for up to 10 users (2GB storage); Standard $5.42/user/month; Premium $10.44/user/month; Enterprise custom-priced. Paid tiers include Atlassian's Rovo AI on a monthly credit allowance (~25/70/150 credits per user across Standard/Premium/Enterprise). (Source: Atlassian pricing pages, aggregated via costbench.com and usecarly.com.)
- Target customer spans SMB through large enterprise; Confluence is often adopted bottom-up by engineering/product teams already on Jira, then expanded company-wide.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: deep native integration with Jira and the broader Atlassian suite (Trello, Bitbucket, Loom), making it the default wiki for organizations already standardized on Atlassian tooling.
- Available as Cloud (multi-tenant SaaS), Data Center (self-managed, being deprecated for new sales), with mobile apps (iOS/Android) and REST/Forge APIs for extensibility via a large marketplace of third-party apps.
- Key features: structured spaces/pages, real-time co-editing, templates, page permissions, whiteboards, and Rovo AI (search, page summarization, content generation) layered across the suite.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed)
- Atlassian does not break out Confluence-specific revenue in its 10-K; it reports revenue by type (Subscription, Maintenance, Other) and disclosed segment commentary, not per-product figures. Atlassian FY2025 (ended June 30, 2025) total revenue was $5.215 billion, up 20% YoY; subscription revenue was $4.931 billion, up 26% YoY (Source: Atlassian FY2025 10-K, SEC EDGAR).
- Confluence revenue is estimated by third-party analysts at roughly $700M–$900M annually (estimated based on Atlassian's historical product-mix disclosures placing Confluence as its second-largest product after Jira, typically ~15-20% of total revenue) — not separately disclosed by Atlassian.
- Atlassian reports 300,000+ customers company-wide across its product portfolio (not Confluence-specific).
- Atlassian has been public since December 2015 (IPO on NASDAQ); no further "funding rounds" apply as a public company. Market cap fluctuates in the $30-50B+ range depending on trading conditions.
- Growth trajectory: Atlassian's Cloud migration push and Data Center price increases have driven double-digit subscription revenue growth; Confluence benefits from cross-sell into the installed Jira base and increasing Rovo AI attach.

## Competitive Position
Confluence is the incumbent wiki/knowledge-base choice for engineering-led organizations already inside the Atlassian ecosystem, giving it a strong distribution moat via Jira bundling that pure-play competitors (Notion, Slab, Nuclino) lack. Its weaknesses are a dated editor/UX reputation and complexity relative to newer, faster tools, which has opened space for Notion and AI-native competitors to win greenfield deals. Atlassian's scale, enterprise sales motion, and Rovo AI investment give Confluence durability even where user experience is not best-in-class.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-seat SaaS subscription (Cloud: Standard/Premium/Enterprise tiers), plus legacy Data Center licensing being phased down.
- **Secondary monetization:** Rovo AI credit add-ons, third-party Marketplace apps (Atlassian takes a revenue share), premium support, and bundling with Jira/other Atlassian products that raises per-account contract value.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Sales-led enterprise for large accounts, blended with self-serve + inside sales (PLG) for SMB/mid-market — Atlassian's core motion is bottom-up freemium signup from Jira-adjacent teams, layered with a direct enterprise sales org for large multi-year deals.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $3,000–$12,000 (estimated) — a wide blended range reflecting the hybrid motion: cheap self-serve signups pull the average down, while enterprise sales-assisted deals (consistent with Atlassian's relatively low sales & marketing spend as a share of revenue vs. peers, a hallmark of PLG-led growth) pull it up.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $15,000–$60,000+ (estimated) — derived from per-seat pricing ($5.42–$10.44/user/month) across typical 20–200+ seat deployments, assuming a 5–6 year average customer lifetime given high switching costs once a team is embedded with Jira.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~5:1 (estimated) — healthy, reflecting Atlassian's efficient GTM enabled by product-led growth and cross-sell into its existing Jira base.
- **User demographics:** Spans SMB through enterprise (100K+ Confluence customers within Atlassian's 300K+ total base); most common company-size band is 1,001–5,000 employees; concentrated in software development/engineering-led organizations already on Jira; global footprint, US/Europe-heavy.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global, North America-led despite Australian founding, top countries: United States (~45%), United Kingdom (~12%), Australia (~8%), Germany (~7%) — estimated from customer base composition and disclosed US/Europe-heavy demographics, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
