# Granola

## Overview
- Independent, privately held, VC-backed startup (developed by Granola Inc., maker of the product also referred to as "Granola AI"); private, not a subsidiary.
- Founded March 2023 by Chris Pedregal (CEO) and Sam Stephenson; headquartered in London, UK.
- Employee count estimate: rapidly scaling; estimated in the 60-120 range as of 2026 following its $125M Series C aimed partly at "expanding its workforce" (estimated based on funding raise commentary; exact figure not disclosed).

## Business Model
- Freemium subscription SaaS, recently simplified/rebranded pricing structure (February 2026).
- Pricing (monthly, no annual discount currently offered): Basic (free) — unlimited meetings but only a rolling 30-day note history; Business $14/user/month — unlimited history, integrations with Notion, HubSpot, Attio, Affinity, Zapier, and API access; Enterprise $35/user/month — SSO, admin controls, usage analytics, org-wide auto-deletion. Source: granola.ai/pricing, costbench.com/software/ai-meeting-assistants/granola.
- Target customer: originally individual knowledge workers/founders; expanding toward team and enterprise buyers as it repositions from "meeting notetaker" to broader "enterprise AI app."

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiators: works from a user's own raw, real-time notes plus the audio transcript (rather than being a passive bot in the call), producing AI-enhanced notes that read like the user wrote them; historically desktop-native (Mac-first) rather than browser/bot-based.
- Platform availability: native macOS and Windows desktop apps; works across Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and in-person conversations via system audio capture.
- Key features: AI chat within and across meetings, shared folders, custom templates, multi-language support, and (as of 2026) an expansion beyond meeting notes into a broader "enterprise AI app" positioning.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: not fully disclosed, but TechCrunch reported the Series C followed "a quarter in which Granola's revenue grew by 250%," indicating high growth off a presumably small but rapidly scaling base (estimated absolute figure not disclosed).
- Users/customers: not precisely disclosed; broad consumer/prosumer adoption implied by rapid valuation growth.
- Funding: Series C of $125M led by Index Ventures, joined by Kleiner Perkins, Lightspeed, Spark, and NFDG, valuing the company at $1.5B — up from a $250M valuation less than a year earlier. Total raised to date: $192M. Source: TechCrunch (techcrunch.com/2026/03/25), Bloomberg, SiliconANGLE.
- Growth trajectory: among the fastest valuation increases in the AI-notetaking category (6x valuation growth in under a year), reflecting strong investor conviction in its expansion from consumer notetaker to enterprise AI platform.

## Competitive Position
- Granola is the clear momentum leader in this category by funding and valuation, using its capital advantage to expand from a single-player notetaking tool into a broader enterprise AI platform play, which could let it out-execute smaller rivals (tl;dv, Grain, Supernormal) on product breadth.
- Its moat today is more about brand momentum, UX polish (writing-alongside-transcript approach), and capital than proprietary technology, since transcription/summarization is broadly commoditized; sustaining its valuation will require successfully cross-selling enterprise features against Zoom AI Companion (free/bundled) and Microsoft/Google's native AI notetaking.
- Faces the structural risk common to all standalone AI notetakers: as video-conferencing platforms bundle equivalent AI summarization for free, Granola's long-term defensibility depends on becoming a genuine cross-tool "enterprise AI app" rather than a single-purpose add-on.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user SaaS subscription (Business/Enterprise tiers).
- **Secondary monetization:** Enterprise tier upsell (SSO, admin controls, usage analytics); API access and integrations (Notion, HubSpot, Attio, Affinity, Zapier) as retention/upsell levers; no ads.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion — strong product-led virality among founders/knowledge workers (Mac-first UX, word-of-mouth) with 250% quarter-over-quarter revenue growth reported, supplemented by an emerging enterprise sales motion as it repositions toward "enterprise AI app."
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $30–$120 (estimated) — heavily organic/PLG-driven consumer-grade growth keeps CAC low, though the shift toward enterprise buyers will raise blended CAC over time.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $250–$600 (estimated) — based on ~$14-35/user/month ARPU and ~18-24 month average retention, with high growth suggesting improving retention as the product matures.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** roughly 5:1 (estimated).
- **User demographics:** Startup founders, knowledge workers, and increasingly enterprise teams; 25-45 age range; UK/US-heavy given London HQ and rapid Western-market adoption.
- **Estimated user geography:** UK/Europe alongside a large US base, top countries: United States (~40%), United Kingdom (~25%), Canada (~9%), Germany (~7%) — estimated from HQ location / platform reach, not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
