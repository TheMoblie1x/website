# MyScript

## Overview
- Privately held, independent French technology company (not a subsidiary), with minority institutional investment; not VC-hypergrowth-backed in the typical sense — funding is modest relative to revenue.
- Founded 1998 (originally as Vision Objects, rebranded around its MyScript product line); HQ in Nantes, France.
- Employee count estimate: ~96 employees (Latka/industry estimate, 2024).

## Business Model
- Multi-channel B2B + B2C: (1) B2B/OEM licensing of its handwriting-recognition SDK and "Interactive Ink" technology to hardware makers (e.g., smart pen/tablet OEMs), software vendors, and automotive manufacturers; (2) direct-to-consumer apps (MyScript Notes/Nebo, MyScript Calculator, MyScript Math); (3) cloud API subscriptions for developers integrating handwriting recognition.
- Pricing: consumer app Nebo is sold via one-time/subscription purchase on app stores (tens of dollars range); SDK/API licensing to OEMs and enterprises is quote-based/custom, not publicly listed — typical of embedded-technology licensing businesses.
- Target customers: OEM hardware partners (styluses, e-ink tablets, smart notebooks), enterprise and education software integrators, automotive manufacturers building in-car handwriting input, and individual consumers using the Nebo note-taking app.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator: decades of R&D in AI-driven handwriting and math recognition ("Interactive Ink"), a mature, widely licensed SDK considered a category leader in accuracy across languages and scripts.
- Platform availability: cross-platform SDK (iOS, Android, Windows, Linux, embedded/RTOS) for OEM integration; consumer apps on iOS, Android, Windows.
- Key features: real-time handwriting-to-text conversion, math equation recognition, diagram/shape recognition, multi-language support, and ink-based interactive editing (as opposed to static handwriting capture) — positioning MyScript as the "engine" behind many third-party note-taking and stylus products rather than a standalone PKM competitor.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: reported at approximately $18M (2024, per Latka), though other estimates (Kona Equity) cite ~$15.8M — figures vary by source and year; treat as approximate.
- Users: not consistently disclosed at a precise figure; the SDK is embedded across numerous OEM products so end-user reach is far larger than direct app installs (estimated to be in the tens of millions of end-users via OEM partners, based on the breadth of licensing deals, not a disclosed count).
- Funding history: relatively small total raised (~$2.7M reported by one source), with investors including Atlanpole, XAnge, Irus Partenaires, Myrian Capital, and DoubleDay Holdings — consistent with a profitable/self-funding B2B licensing business rather than a venture-scale growth story.
- Growth trajectory: long-established (25+ years) and profitable-oriented licensing business; growth is steady/incremental rather than hypergrowth, tied to OEM design-win cycles and enterprise digitization of handwriting workflows.

## Competitive Position
MyScript holds a strong, largely uncontested position as the leading independent handwriting/ink-recognition technology licensor — most competitors in this niche (Wacom's internal tech, MyScript alternatives from big tech like Apple Scribble or Samsung) are either bundled into a single hardware ecosystem or less mature. Its moat is deep IP and multi-language recognition accuracy built over 25+ years, plus entrenched OEM relationships that are costly to switch away from. It is not a direct competitor to consumer PKM apps like Roam or Notion, but rather an enabling technology layer many of them and hardware makers build on top of.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** B2B/OEM SDK licensing ("Interactive Ink") to hardware makers, software vendors, and automotive manufacturers — the core of a ~$18M-revenue, 25+ year-old profitable business.
- **Secondary monetization:** Direct-to-consumer apps (Nebo, MyScript Calculator/Math) sold via one-time/subscription app-store purchase, plus developer-facing cloud API subscriptions for handwriting recognition — smaller revenue lines that also serve as a funnel/demonstration channel for the OEM SDK business.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Sales-led enterprise (B2B SDK licensing) — MyScript wins OEM "design-in" deals with hardware/automotive partners through direct enterprise sales and long technical evaluation cycles, while the consumer apps grow via App Store organic search and word-of-mouth.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $10,000–$50,000+ per enterprise/OEM design-win (estimated) — typical of deal-based B2B SDK licensing with multi-month technical integration and sales-engineering involvement; consumer app CAC is much lower, $5–$20 (estimated), driven by organic App Store discovery.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $100,000–$500,000+ per OEM/enterprise licensing relationship (estimated), reflecting multi-year embedded-SDK contracts with recurring royalties across a hardware product's lifecycle; consumer app LTV is modest, roughly $15–$40 (estimated) given low one-time/subscription price points.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~8:1 (estimated) on the OEM/enterprise side ($250K mid LTV / $30K mid CAC), a healthy ratio typical of sticky embedded-technology licensing once a design-win is secured; consumer-app ratio is roughly 2:1, thinner but low-cost to sustain.
- **User demographics:** Two distinct segments — (1) enterprise/OEM buyers: hardware product managers and engineering teams at stylus/tablet, e-ink, and automotive manufacturers (B2B, global, no strong age skew); (2) end consumers of Nebo/MyScript apps: students, professionals, and stylus-tablet owners (e.g., reMarkable, Samsung Note users) roughly 20–50, skewing toward productivity-focused knowledge workers. Inferred from OEM partner profile and consumer app positioning rather than disclosed demographic data.
- **Estimated user geography:** Europe-centric B2B with global OEM reach, top countries: France (~20%), United States (~25%), Germany (~10%), Japan (~8%), South Korea (~7%) — estimated from Nantes/French HQ, European B2B enterprise base, and OEM partnerships with global hardware/automotive manufacturers, not disclosed.
