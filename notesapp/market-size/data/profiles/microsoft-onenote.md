# Microsoft OneNote

## Overview
- Parent company: Microsoft Corporation (public, NASDAQ: MSFT). OneNote is a product within Microsoft's Productivity and Business Processes segment (Microsoft 365 family), not an independent subsidiary.
- Founded / launched: OneNote originally launched in 2003 as part of Office 2003. Microsoft was founded in 1975 by Bill Gates and Paul Allen; HQ in Redmond, Washington.
- Employee count: Microsoft has roughly 228,000 employees company-wide (FY2024 10-K); no separate headcount is disclosed for the OneNote team, which sits within the Office/Microsoft 365 engineering organization.

## Business Model
- Freemium bundled into Microsoft's productivity ecosystem: a free version of OneNote (with 5GB OneDrive storage) is available on Windows, Mac, iOS, Android, and web; full-featured access is bundled with Microsoft 365 subscriptions.
- Pricing (US, as of 2026, per Microsoft/reseller listings): Microsoft 365 Personal $9.99/month (or ~$69.99/year), Family $12.99/month, Premium tier $19.99/month; Business Basic $7.20/user/month, Business Standard $15/user/month — OneNote desktop/web app is included in all these tiers rather than sold standalone.
- Target customer: broad — consumers, students, and enterprise/business users, since OneNote is positioned as a general-purpose digital notebook bundled across both consumer and commercial Microsoft 365 SKUs.

## Product & Positioning
- Differentiators: a free-form "digital notebook" canvas (type or write anywhere on the page, unlike linear document editors), deep integration with Outlook, Teams, and the rest of Office, notebook/section/page hierarchical organization, and strong inking/handwriting support on Windows touch and Surface devices.
- Platform availability: Windows, macOS, iOS, Android, and web (OneNote for the web); broadest cross-platform footprint among the "big tech bundled" note apps in this set.
- Key features: multi-user real-time collaboration on shared notebooks, OneDrive/SharePoint-backed sync, audio/video note embedding, tagging and search across handwritten and typed content, and Copilot-powered AI summarization/organization features rolling out across Microsoft 365 apps.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Revenue: N/A as a standalone figure — OneNote revenue is not broken out; it rolls into Microsoft's Office Commercial/Consumer products and Microsoft 365 subscription revenue within the Productivity and Business Processes segment, which generated roughly $77.7 billion in FY2024 (Microsoft 10-K). OneNote's specific contribution is not disclosed.
- Users: estimated in the hundreds of millions, based on Microsoft 365's reported base of roughly 87 million consumer subscribers and hundreds of millions of commercial seats (Microsoft investor disclosures); OneNote itself does not report a separate active-user figure, and it is also available for free outside any subscription.
- Funding history: N/A — organically developed and wholly owned by Microsoft, a public company; no external funding applies.
- Growth trajectory: growth is tied to Microsoft 365 subscription growth rather than an independent OneNote metric; recent investment has focused on Copilot integration and cross-device sync reliability rather than new standalone OneNote monetization.

## Competitive Position
- OneNote's moat is enterprise and Office-suite lock-in: it is the default notebook app for any organization standardized on Microsoft 365, giving it strong penetration in education and corporate environments where Teams/Outlook/SharePoint integration matters.
- Its broad platform availability (unlike Apple Notes) makes it a credible cross-platform alternative to Evernote and Notion, though its free-canvas UX is sometimes seen as less structured/searchable than linear or database-style competitors.
- Long-term position depends less on note-taking feature parity and more on how deeply Copilot AI features get woven into the Microsoft 365 bundle, reinforcing OneNote's role as a retention feature rather than a standalone revenue driver.

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Bundled subscription revenue via Microsoft 365 (consumer and commercial SKUs); OneNote itself is not sold standalone.
- **Secondary monetization:** Upsell path into higher Microsoft 365 tiers (Premium, Business Standard+) and Copilot AI add-ons; enterprise seat expansion via Business/Enterprise plans; no ads, no separate OneNote purchase.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Organic + freemium conversion for consumers (free OneNote app funnels into Microsoft 365 upsell) blended with sales-led enterprise (Microsoft field/partner sales driving Microsoft 365 Business/Enterprise seats where OneNote rides along).
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $150–$600 (estimated) blended — near-$0 for the free standalone app (organic/pre-installed on Windows), but meaningfully higher when attributing a share of Microsoft's enterprise sales & marketing spend to a Microsoft 365 seat that happens to use OneNote.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $200–$900 (estimated) — e.g., ~$10/mo consumer Microsoft 365 subscription × ~36-month average retention ≈ $360, with business seats (~$15–$20/user/month × multi-year tenure) pulling the range higher.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~1.5:1 (estimated) — modest and below the healthy 3:1 bar when CAC is fully loaded with enterprise sales cost, though this understates OneNote's true contribution since it's a retention feature riding on a much larger, already-justified Microsoft 365 purchase decision.
- **User demographics:** Broad — students and knowledge workers in the consumer tier, enterprise/IT-managed users in the commercial tier; skews toward Windows/Surface-heavy organizations and education (schools using Microsoft 365 Education). Inferred from Microsoft 365's disclosed subscriber base rather than OneNote-specific data.
- **Estimated user geography:** Global/Distributed, mirroring Microsoft 365's enterprise/education footprint, top countries: United States (~25%), India (~10%), United Kingdom (~7%), Germany (~6%), Japan (~5%) — estimated from HQ location and platform reach (bundled into Windows/Office worldwide), not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
