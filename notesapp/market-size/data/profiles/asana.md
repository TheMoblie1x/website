# Asana

## Overview
- Public: Asana, Inc. (NYSE: ASAN).
- Founded in 2008 by Dustin Moskovitz (Facebook co-founder) and Justin Rosenstein; HQ in San Francisco, CA.
- Employee count is roughly 1,500-1,700 as of recent filings (down from prior years following cost-reduction efforts); exact current figure available in the most recent 10-K.

## Business Model
- Per-seat SaaS subscription model for a work/project management platform with tiered plans; Asana includes a free tier and paid tiers priced per user per month.
- Published pricing (2025-2026): Personal (free), Starter (~$10.99/user/month billed annually), Advanced (~$24.99/user/month billed annually), and custom-priced Enterprise/Enterprise+ tiers; AI Studio and workflow-automation features are increasingly positioned as premium add-ons.
- Target customer ranges from small teams (free/Starter) to large enterprises (Enterprise+), with "Core customers" defined by Asana as those spending $5,000+ annually — a cohort the company tracks explicitly as its primary growth metric.

## Product & Positioning
- Core differentiator is structured work/project management (tasks, projects, portfolios, workflows, goals) with strong cross-functional visibility (timeline, board, list, calendar views), rather than a freeform docs tool — Asana's "docs" angle is secondary, mostly task descriptions and project briefs rather than a standalone document product.
- Available via web, desktop (Mac/Windows), and mobile (iOS/Android) apps, with extensive third-party integrations (Slack, Salesforce, Adobe, Microsoft Teams).
- Positioned as a work-management-of-record platform competing with Monday.com and ClickUp; recent strategic emphasis is on "AI teammates" and AI Studio for workflow automation, aiming to differentiate on enterprise-grade governance and structured process management.

## Financial Profile (estimates where not disclosed — always label as "estimated")
- Disclosed revenue: $723.9 million for fiscal year 2025 (ended January 2025), up 11% year-over-year — a company-reported, audited figure.
- Disclosed Core customers ($5,000+ ARR) reached 24,062 in Q4 FY2025, up 11% year-over-year; the platform has crossed over 170,000 total customers worldwide. Dollar-based net retention was 96% in Q4 FY2025, indicating some expansion/contraction headwinds typical of the broader SaaS market in this period.
- Asana is public (IPO via direct listing in 2020); market cap was approximately $2.0-2.3 billion as of September 2026 — a significant compression from its 2021 highs, reflecting slowing growth and competitive/AI-disruption concerns. The company has begun disclosing early AI-product ARR (over $6 million exiting fiscal 2026) as a forward growth signal.

## Competitive Position
- Asana is a well-established, publicly traded leader in structured work/project management, but growth has decelerated meaningfully (low double-digit revenue growth, compressed net retention) amid intensifying competition from Monday.com, ClickUp, and AI-native workflow tools.
- Its moat is enterprise process maturity, integration breadth, and brand recognition among cross-functional teams, but it lacks the flexible docs/database hybrid appeal of Notion/Coda and faces real pricing/feature pressure from faster-growing private competitors like ClickUp.
- The market has priced in real uncertainty about Asana's ability to reaccelerate growth, reflected in its market cap decline since its 2021 peak; its path forward is explicitly tied to successful AI feature monetization (AI Studio, AI teammates).

## Unit Economics & Monetization

- **Primary revenue source:** Per-user SaaS subscription — Starter, Advanced, and Enterprise tiers priced per member/month, billed annually or monthly.
- **Secondary monetization:** AI Studio/AI teammates add-on, Enterprise-tier governance/security features, and platform/app integrations marketplace.
- **Growth/acquisition model:** Self-serve + inside sales (PLG), with growing enterprise field-sales motion — Asana historically grew bottoms-up via free-tier team adoption, now layered with a dedicated enterprise sales org to land larger cross-functional deployments and defend against ClickUp/Monday.com.
- **Estimated CAC (customer acquisition cost):** $1,000–$3,500 (estimated) — public-company scale PLG+sales blend, reflecting significant sales & marketing spend (historically ~45-50% of revenue) needed to sustain growth against well-funded private competitors.
- **Estimated LTV (customer lifetime value):** $4,000–$12,000 (estimated) — assuming ACV in the $1,000-3,000+ range for expanding enterprise accounts and an average customer lifetime of roughly 4-5 years, tempered by recent net-retention compression.
- **LTV:CAC ratio (implied):** ~3.5:1 (estimated).
- **User demographics:** Mid-market to enterprise cross-functional teams (marketing, ops, IT, product) at established companies; skews North America/Europe with strong presence among larger, process-mature organizations.
- **Estimated user geography:** North America, top countries: United States (~55%), Japan (~9%), United Kingdom (~8%), Australia (~6%), Germany (~5%) — estimated from HQ location (San Francisco) and 10-K geographic revenue disclosure (~55% US / ~45% international), not disclosed.

*All figures in this section are estimates based on business-model archetype and publicly available pricing — none of these companies disclose actual CAC/LTV. Treat as directional only.*
